Introduction and specs

Introduction

It's the end of July already, and in keeping with annual tradition, Samsung unpacked its latest crop of foldables. The big novelty this year is the inclusion of a whole new foldable form factor. We aren't sure the industry has settled on one clean and concise name for it yet, but it is this book-style foldable with a 4:3 inner display.

You probably already heard this in some shape or form, but Samsung decided that rather than carving out a new name for this new foldable form factor, it is going to go down the admittedly confusing route of naming the Galaxy Z Fold8. So, if you are looking for the Z Fold7 successor, that would actually be the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra. Confusing? Perhaps. A truly big deal? Well, probably not. We can't imagine many prospective buyers of either device will actually end up getting the wrong thing by mistake. I will probably make our lives a bit harder when discussing the models in a historical context, but that's hardly an issue worth considering.

As for the Flip - it is all fine and safe. It continues to be called the Flip, currently the Flip8, and there is no FE model this time around. There are no major hardware advancements to speak of aside from the move to an Exynos 2600 like the one used in the Galaxy S26. The phone is ever so slightly thinner too.

Samsung's foldables still end up occupying this odd transitional time of year, which is pretty close to the tail end of the current flagship chipset lifecycle, which does tend to rub some people the wrong way. We get that. It almost feels like you are dropping a huge sum of money to get soon-to-be-replaced hardware. But that's a pretty harsh way of looking at things in our opinion. Yes, this new crop of Galaxy foldables is all based on the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 and the Samsung Exynos 2600. But both will continue being excellent chips in a few months when they ultimately get "replaced".

That said, there are still some improvements to consider in each of the two returning models. Plus, this year, a whole new Galaxy Z Fold8 to explore. So, let's dig into our initial impressions.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold 8 Ultra hands-on review

One thing struck out for us, and that's the fact Samsung has increased the battery capacity by more than 10% while the weight of the phone has remained the same as the Z Fold7. Samsung has achieved that by integrating Si/C (Silicon-Carbon) tech for the phone's battery for the first time.

Now, without formally testing the battery endurance, we can't say how much of a difference the increase in battery capacity will actually make. However, we do have leaked EU energy labels for the Z Fold8 Ultra. It shows 51:00 hours of battery endurance for the Z Fold8 Ultra. In comparison, the same rating for the Z Fold7 is 40:28h. That constitutes around 20% better battery life. This sounds like a much-needed improvement for the Galaxy Fold line, since with an Active Use Score of 11:44h, the Z Fold7 placed notably below its sibling fold competitors back when we tested it.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra

Before we officially move on from the battery system, we have to mention the charging power rating has finally been upgraded to 45W. Yes, surprisingly as it may be, up until this year the best the Galaxy Fold line could offer was 25W charging. We expect notably faster charging on this model. Samsung advertises around 67% charge in thirty minutes.

Samsung also mentioned something it calls a "new dual-path charging architecture" that supposedly helps "distribute power more efficiently across the battery system for faster, more stable and reliable charging throughout the day". In reality, the phone can charge both batteries simultaneously inside the phone.

Let's take a step back once again and look at how the Z Fold8 Ultra has changed externally - honestly, not at all. The Z Fold8 Ultra is remarkably similar to its predecessor. That includes pretty flat sides, as is the current trend, but also surprisingly boxy corners, while most current smartphone design seems to go for big, swooping round edges.

The Z Fold8 Ultra is available in the two common colors shared across the entire Galaxy Z lineup - Graphite and Cream. The Z Fold8 Ultra in particular also gets a Violet Shadow variant, and there is a Samsung.com exclusive Green Shadow variant. Violet Shadow is the hero color, and it is the one we got to see in person; honestly, it looks great - we only wish the Flip8 shared the same color too - it's much nicer than Graphite.

Samsung hasn't revealed any major changes to the hinge mechanism, but they've expanded on last year's flexible Titanium plate supporting the foldable screen by incorporating an extra flexible Titanium-alloy sheet inside the panel itself and they are calling the whole approach as "Flex Titanium" technology. The change should make the panel more resilient, especially to pressure and impact, and Samsung also claims it makes the whole assembly thinner somehow.

Samsung's press release also claims that the new construction is meant to "reduce crease visibility". However, that is not our experience. The crease was actually quite prominent on our demo unit. Definitely more so than on the Z Fold8 demo unit.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra display crease

Now this could simply be due to the Z Fold8 Ultra unit having seen more open and close cycles, so we'll give them the benefit of the doubt, as the Z Fold8 demo unit had no noticeable crease whatsoever.

Now another thing Samsung says it has worked on is display tension and magnetic force to make unfolding feel "smoother, lighter and more natural". Samsung calls this an "Easy Open-and-Close Mechanism", apparently at least in part borrowed from the South Korea-exclusive Galaxy Z TriFold. We can confirm that this is indeed a thing we noticed. In practice, we found it noticeably easier to separate the two halves of the phone for the first 5mm or so. In that range, the hinge doesn't seem to offer any resistance and the magnets are easier to overcome without requiring great force. Even so, opening the phone up with one hand is still very hard and probably impractical for most users.

Speaking of the display setup on the Z Fold8 Ultra, we don't think anything major has changed really. The outside display appears to be identical to last year's model. The inside display is perhaps a bit less reflective, though it is pretty hard to say, so if there is some new coating, it seems rather mild. Samsung does claim an improved maximum brightness of 3,000 nits this year, up from the official 2,600 nits figure from last year. That's hardly a meaningful improvement in practice, but still noteworthy. We'll definitely do our best to verify the claim in the full review.

Samsung did improve the main display resolution this year, bumping it up to 2,504 x 2,256 pixels, which at 8 inches in diagonal works out to around 422 ppi. By comparison, the Z Fold7 has a resolution of 1968 x 2184 pixels on its 8-inch panel and around 368 ppi. That's not an insignificant resolution upgrade, but we still don't consider it major since it's not a night-and-day difference in person.

Presumably the refresh rate control hasn't changed, with LTPO tech on both panels. Of course, Samsung's Vision Booster is present, though it is definitely not a new thing.

As mentioned, not a lot has changed about the rest of the phone. One thing already hinted at by the Ultra moniker is that Samsung still views this traditional foldable form factor as its "top" offering in the space, for lack of a better term. It gets the biggest battery and the fastest charging. Although the new form factor Z Fold8 also gets the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 chipset, Samsung specifically mentions that: "Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra uses an expanded graphite cooling structure engineered for improved thermal conductivity, helping dissipate heat more effectively and keep the device responsive during demanding tasks."

That does make us wonder whether, perhaps, the new Z Fold8 is expected not to deliver the full performance of its sibling? Perhaps the newer form factor is harder to properly cool? Of course, these are merely speculations on our part before we get to actually test the pair properly.

We can't really say much about the camera system prior to formal testing either. Samsung says that the Z Fold8 Ultra gets an upgraded 50MP ultrawide this year. It certainly looks better on paper than the 12MP unit on the Z Fold7, with more resolution, a brighter lens and a slightly bigger sensor. That said, we don't actually expect any major quality improvements, just like we didn't really see any major improvements when the S25 Ultra first got this "ultrawide upgrade".

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra camera UI

All in all, the Z Fold8 Ultra looks to represent a pretty modest upgrade, but an upgrade nonetheless. Plus, it is still a solid foldable through and through.

Regardless of what we collectively decide to start calling this form factor, there seems to be an undeniable push towards it. The big driving force behind it, of course, is expected to be the upcoming iPhone. The iPhone Ultra, if rumors are anything to go by. It will be Apple's first foldable, and it is expected to offer a similar aspect ratio on its displays. Huawei already has a China-only Pura X Max in this form factor, and we expect other major industry players to follow suit sooner rather than later, definitely on an accelerated timeline once the iPhone becomes a reality. That's just how the wheel usually turns.

One thing potentially worth reiterating before we dig into things is that this is technically not Samsung's "flagship foldable", whatever that combination of words might mean to you. What we mean is that certain expectations must be tempered and we have to be fine with getting an experience that is sort of "almost on the level" of the Z Fold8 Ultra. That means things like no telephoto camera and technically smaller display. Though, they are so different that that point is debatable.

On about the new form factor. It is almost the same size as a passport, which is pretty standardized and the best proverbial "banana for reference" we can offer you right now. We have to say that it quickly grew on us for several reasons, which we will go over. But, almost instantly, we appreciated how easily "pocketable" it is.

It also feels a lot lighter than the Z Fold8 Ultra because it is at just 201 grams. It is technically slightly thicker than the Z Fold8 Ultra, but it's really not noticeably in practice.

Samsung definitely did its best to keep the foldable design as consistent as possible. You get the same flat sides and the same general I/O placement. The displays look boxy here as well, also going against the common industry trend of large rounded corners.

Samsung Galazy Z Fold8

The build seems to be comparable, and although we are yet to get the formal bill of materials confirmation we would ideally want, we believe that the glass on both sides is the same as on the Z Fold8 Ultra and so is the aluminum on the frame. The IP48 ingress protection is certainly shared among Samsung's latest foldable trio.

Before we move on to some actual usability notes, let's look at the color selection real quick. The Z Fold8 is available in the same "standard" Graphite and Cream as the rest of the 2026 Galaxy Z lineup. The hero color is called Lavender, and the Samsung.com exclusive color is Pistachio.

Our demo unit was in Cream, though it didn't seem to have the warm tinge you'd expect to come with the name Cream, and if there is any, it's all but lost in our photos.

Let's talk display, since that is the real novelty factor here. The outside one measures 5.5 inches in diagonal, which is technically smaller than the 6.5-inch panel on the Z Fold8 Ultra, though with a vastly different aspect ratio. The Z Fold8 Ultra has a FullHD+ resolution of 1080 x 2520 pixels on its outside display, which works out to a neat, even if not-exactly-popular 3:7 aspect ratio. It's the definition of "tall and skinny".

In contrast, the Z Fold8 cover display has a resolution of 1,248 x 1,972 pixels, which Samsung calls WUXGA+ and, at first glance, it does seem deceptively higher than the one on the Z Fold8 Ultra, though, in reality, both panels have very similar pixel density figures. The aspect ratio on the Z Fold8 outside display works out to an awkward 312:493, which is quite close to the 10:16 Samsung advertises.

Honestly, it seems very odd at first glance in person. It's not an aspect ratio most of us have really encountered at this size. Though, it arguably makes more practical sense than the "tall and skinny" panel on the Z Fold8 Ultra. Reaching from corner to corner with one hand was not a challenge for us, which is important.

We found usability great here, especially when it came to typing. We definitely found typing on the wide cover display of the Samsung easier than on the Honor Magic 8 Pro. And the iPhone 17 Pro, which we had handy for a side-by-side comparison, only managed to keep up in typing due to its overall superior keyboard layout and programming.

Left: Apple iPhone 17 Pro • Right: Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8

Interestingly, the iPhone 17 Pro did seem to fit more text on a single screen, but the odd aspect on the Z Fold8's cover screen really managed to shine with social media content like Instagram, where we could maximize our screen real estate.

So, we found the cover display to be pretty great in practice. What about the main foldable display? Well, it is a smaller 7.6-inch display with lower resolution than the Z Fold8 Ultra's main display. Though 1,848 x 2,448 pixels is still perfectly sharp, working out to about 403 ppi of density.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 main display productivity

The display on the Z Fold8 is 12 pixels too tall to be a perfect 4:3 ratio. So for all practical intents & purposes, we can round it to a 4:3 aspect ratio when discussing it.

Anyway, do you enjoy some old-school animated shows? Well, this is about as perfect an experience as you can get. Just us? Well, don't worry, this particular aspect is also a boon for multitasking, with two apps open on either side of the display.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra and Z Fold8 16:9 video playback comparison

We were also kind of interested to know how watching a video in the much more popular 16:9 aspect on the Z Fold8 compared to doing the same on the Z Fold8 Ultra. Well, actually, surprisingly well. Lining the two up as best as possible, the Z Fold8 Ultra does show a slightly larger image, but also much bigger black bars, for what that's worth.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra and Z Fold8 compared

Honestly, we think that this aspect ratio and form factor make more sense overall. Samsung says that both displays are more comfortable and natural regardless of the task, whether it be video watching, gaming or reading. A personal opinion, of course, since it will come down to preference.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 camera viewfinder

Honestly, the one thing that looked really off to us on both screens was the camera viewfinder. Anything outside of Portrait Photo mode just seems awkward, but that's something that you could get used to, we guess.

Other than that, it is perhaps good to note that both panels on the Z Fold8 can refresh at up to 120Hz and seem to both be based on LTPO tech that offers the same dynamic refresh rate switching as on the Z Fold8 Ultra.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 minimal display crease

For what it's worth, unlike the Z Fold8 Ultra demo unit we were handed, the Z Fold8 barely had a visible display crease.

Samsung seems to be using very similar tech for the hinges on both phones. The Flex Titanium technology chain we already described in detail in the Z Fold8 Ultra hands-on is here as well. That means an extra flexible titanium-alloy sheet is incorporated inside the display itself and presumably a thinner overall assembly with better durability.

Also, the hinge promises the same improvements to stability and less force required to open the magnetic latch.

While technically the Z Fold8 isn't quite as "feature-packed" as the Z Fold8 Ultra, there are very few actual "shortcomings" to point to. We already mentioned the lack of a dedicated telephoto camera, which is probably the one big omission. Anything past 2x zoom just looked a bit "fuzzy" while shooting from the main 50MP camera. Said 50MP camera appears to be very similar to the one inside the Galaxy S26, which is to say pretty good, but far from industry-leading. Of course, we will reserve any quality judgment for the full review.

The battery inside the Z Fold8 is technically a bit smaller than the Ultra's, rated at 4,800 mAh typical capacity. There is actually still a bit of a debate as of writing this hands-on whether this pack is Si/C like the Ultra's or regular Li-Po.

Regardless, we find it much more meaningful that Samsung included the same 45W charging support on the regular Z Fold8. It promises a 63% charge in around 30 minutes.

As already mentioned, the Z Fold8 uses the same Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 chipset as the Z Fold8 Ultra. Though, based on some of Samsung's wording in the press release, said chip might not be cooled quite as effectively in this form factor. Of course, this is more of a speculation at this point, and we look forward to actually confirming performance and stability in the full review.

All things considered, we very much enjoyed our short initial time with the Z Fold8 and believe it might just have a bright future ahead of it.

Design-wise, it is pretty much the same device. The new model is supposedly minimally thinner (like 0.4mm when closed) and something like 8 grams lighter. Other than that - same familiar shape and size.

While it doesn't stand out compared to the rest of the Galaxy Z crowd, we wouldn't exactly say it fits the exact same design mold neatly. The Z Flip8 has generally softer lines with a lot more rounding here and there. Certainly in the corners of both the device and its displays. It is not nearly as "boxy".

While probably purposefully a bit vague about things, it seems that Samsung's quoted upgraded brightness figure of 3,000 nits applies to all three new Galaxy Z devices, including the Z Flip8. So, there's an upgrade for you - around 400 nits more at peak output than last year's model. Still seemingly no LTPO on the cover display, in case anyone was hoping for that in particular.

Also, presumably, Samsung applied similar tuning to the hinge and the magnets to make the opening smoother and easier. We can't say that we noticed it on the Z Flip8 demo unit quite as much as we did on the Z Fold8 Ultra one. So, it's another "take it or leave it" situation.

Other than that - seemingly the same display with the same resolution. Still a noticeable crease on the inside panel.

Samsung Galaxy Z Flip8

The color selection this year includes the Graphite and Cream shared among all the new Galaxy Z family, a Pink hero color and a Samsung.com exclusive Mint color. We got the frankly boring Graphite color on the demo unit.

No battery or charging upgrades here - still the same 4,300 mAh and 25W charging support. Samsung didn't go through the effort to unify charging across the lineup at 45W.

No camera upgrades either: a 50MP main unit and a 12MP ultrawide from what we can tell - the exact same units as last year.

The chipset has been refreshed from the Exynos 2500 to the newer Exynos 2600. We frankly don't expect a massive performance difference as a result, so perhaps we can hope for some battery life improvements from the better manufacturing node and other hardware and software optimizations.

Honestly, the Z Flip8 left us thoroughly underwhelmed. Last year's Z Flip7 will almost certainly be a better and smarter buy at its lower price point.

Software improvements, conclusion

Software improvements

It is hardly a secret that One UI 9 is right around the corner, so it was only logical and perfectly expected that the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra, Z Fold8 and Z Flip8 would launch with One UI 9 and Android 17 underneath.

Samsung didn't particularly highlight anything revolutionary we haven't heard of coming with One UI 9, instead focusing on iterative improvements on things like Now Brief and Now Nudge, promising ongoing privacy and security in an increasingly more agentic AI smartphone environment and also streamlined integration with its new wearables - the Galaxy Watch Ultra2 and Galaxy Watch9 series as well as the upcoming Intelligent Eyewear.

And speaking of agentic AI, as promised, Gemini Intelligence is apparently right around the corner. For anyone not familiar, it promises an almost frightening amount of AI automation and autonomy through the ability to do things like launch and control apps in the background by itself and execute actions on the user's behalf. Through Google and Samsung's ongoing partnership, we fully expect to experience it on Samsung hardware first.

Apparently Samsung SmartSwitch, which has always been one of the better industry solutions for migrating to a new phone, especially from an older Samsung to a newer one, is getting increasingly better with improved support for iOS to Android migration, which sounds intriguing.

In lieu of any meaningful hardware improvements, Samsung did particularly point out a few software ones to look forward to on the Flip8. Apparently there is a new FlexWindow system, which we didn't get to properly test for ourselves yet. As per Samsung's press release, the new FlexWindow "brings apps, insights and actions directly to the cover screen" and also "makes automation through Gemini Intelligence easier to access from the FlexWindow". There is apparently a new Now Brief implementation for FlexWindow as well.

Final thoughts

Overall, Samsung's new crop of Galaxy Z devices turned out to be an intriguing mix of exciting and underwhelming. We definitely found the new Galaxy Z Fold8 form factor very intriguing. There is still a lot of testing to be done and various particulars to be looked into, but on the surface, as far as first impressions go, we kind of like where things are going.

While decidedly less-exciting, the Z Fold8 Ultra does manage to offer some, even if few, upgrades over its predecessor. The battery capacity increase and faster charging are particularly intriguing, but it remains to be seen just how much of a difference they make. The rest is mostly iterative, but it is iterating on an already solid foundation, so we can't complain.

What we can complain about is the new Galaxy Z Flip8. Honestly, it seems that Samsung straight out gave up on this particular model. Whatever the reason behind that decision might be, frankly, the only reason to get excited about the Z Flip8 is that it has the potential to make the Z Flip7 more affordable.

  • Front camera: Wide (main): 10 MP, f/2.2, 18mm, 1/3.0", 1.12µm; Wide (main): 10 MP, f/2.2, 24mm, 1/3.0", 1.12µm;.
  • Video capture: Rear camera: 8K@30fps, 4K@60fps, 1080p@60/120/240fps (gyro-EIS), 10-bit HDR, HDR10+; Front camera: 4K@30/60fps, 1080p@30/60fps, gyro-EIS.
  • Battery: 5000mAh Si/C battery; 45W wired, PD3.0, 67% in 30 min, 20W wireless, reverse wireless.
  • Connectivity: 5G; eSIM; Wi-Fi 7; BT 6.0, aptX HD; NFC.
  • Misc: Fingerprint reader (side-mounted); stereo speakers; Samsung DeX (desktop experience support), Ultra Wideband (UWB) support.

One thing struck out for us, and that's the fact Samsung has increased the battery capacity by more than 10% while the weight of the phone has remained the same as the Z Fold7. Samsung has achieved that by integrating Si/C (Silicon-Carbon) tech for the phone's battery for the first time.

Now, without formally testing the battery endurance, we can't say how much of a difference the increase in battery capacity will actually make. However, we do have leaked EU energy labels for the Z Fold8 Ultra. It shows 51:00 hours of battery endurance for the Z Fold8 Ultra. In comparison, the same rating for the Z Fold7 is 40:28h. That constitutes around 20% better battery life. This sounds like a much-needed improvement for the Galaxy Fold line, since with an Active Use Score of 11:44h, the Z Fold7 placed notably below its sibling fold competitors back when we tested it.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra

Before we officially move on from the battery system, we have to mention the charging power rating has finally been upgraded to 45W. Yes, surprisingly as it may be, up until this year the best the Galaxy Fold line could offer was 25W charging. We expect notably faster charging on this model. Samsung advertises around 67% charge in thirty minutes.

Samsung also mentioned something it calls a "new dual-path charging architecture" that supposedly helps "distribute power more efficiently across the battery system for faster, more stable and reliable charging throughout the day". In reality, the phone can charge both batteries simultaneously inside the phone.

Let's take a step back once again and look at how the Z Fold8 Ultra has changed externally - honestly, not at all. The Z Fold8 Ultra is remarkably similar to its predecessor. That includes pretty flat sides, as is the current trend, but also surprisingly boxy corners, while most current smartphone design seems to go for big, swooping round edges.

The Z Fold8 Ultra is available in the two common colors shared across the entire Galaxy Z lineup - Graphite and Cream. The Z Fold8 Ultra in particular also gets a Violet Shadow variant, and there is a Samsung.com exclusive Green Shadow variant. Violet Shadow is the hero color, and it is the one we got to see in person; honestly, it looks great - we only wish the Flip8 shared the same color too - it's much nicer than Graphite.

Samsung hasn't revealed any major changes to the hinge mechanism, but they've expanded on last year's flexible Titanium plate supporting the foldable screen by incorporating an extra flexible Titanium-alloy sheet inside the panel itself and they are calling the whole approach as "Flex Titanium" technology. The change should make the panel more resilient, especially to pressure and impact, and Samsung also claims it makes the whole assembly thinner somehow.

Samsung's press release also claims that the new construction is meant to "reduce crease visibility". However, that is not our experience. The crease was actually quite prominent on our demo unit. Definitely more so than on the Z Fold8 demo unit.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra display crease

Now this could simply be due to the Z Fold8 Ultra unit having seen more open and close cycles, so we'll give them the benefit of the doubt, as the Z Fold8 demo unit had no noticeable crease whatsoever.

Now another thing Samsung says it has worked on is display tension and magnetic force to make unfolding feel "smoother, lighter and more natural". Samsung calls this an "Easy Open-and-Close Mechanism", apparently at least in part borrowed from the South Korea-exclusive Galaxy Z TriFold. We can confirm that this is indeed a thing we noticed. In practice, we found it noticeably easier to separate the two halves of the phone for the first 5mm or so. In that range, the hinge doesn't seem to offer any resistance and the magnets are easier to overcome without requiring great force. Even so, opening the phone up with one hand is still very hard and probably impractical for most users.

Speaking of the display setup on the Z Fold8 Ultra, we don't think anything major has changed really. The outside display appears to be identical to last year's model. The inside display is perhaps a bit less reflective, though it is pretty hard to say, so if there is some new coating, it seems rather mild. Samsung does claim an improved maximum brightness of 3,000 nits this year, up from the official 2,600 nits figure from last year. That's hardly a meaningful improvement in practice, but still noteworthy. We'll definitely do our best to verify the claim in the full review.

Samsung did improve the main display resolution this year, bumping it up to 2,504 x 2,256 pixels, which at 8 inches in diagonal works out to around 422 ppi. By comparison, the Z Fold7 has a resolution of 1968 x 2184 pixels on its 8-inch panel and around 368 ppi. That's not an insignificant resolution upgrade, but we still don't consider it major since it's not a night-and-day difference in person.

Presumably the refresh rate control hasn't changed, with LTPO tech on both panels. Of course, Samsung's Vision Booster is present, though it is definitely not a new thing.

As mentioned, not a lot has changed about the rest of the phone. One thing already hinted at by the Ultra moniker is that Samsung still views this traditional foldable form factor as its "top" offering in the space, for lack of a better term. It gets the biggest battery and the fastest charging. Although the new form factor Z Fold8 also gets the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 chipset, Samsung specifically mentions that: "Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra uses an expanded graphite cooling structure engineered for improved thermal conductivity, helping dissipate heat more effectively and keep the device responsive during demanding tasks."

That does make us wonder whether, perhaps, the new Z Fold8 is expected not to deliver the full performance of its sibling? Perhaps the newer form factor is harder to properly cool? Of course, these are merely speculations on our part before we get to actually test the pair properly.

We can't really say much about the camera system prior to formal testing either. Samsung says that the Z Fold8 Ultra gets an upgraded 50MP ultrawide this year. It certainly looks better on paper than the 12MP unit on the Z Fold7, with more resolution, a brighter lens and a slightly bigger sensor. That said, we don't actually expect any major quality improvements, just like we didn't really see any major improvements when the S25 Ultra first got this "ultrawide upgrade".

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra camera UI

All in all, the Z Fold8 Ultra looks to represent a pretty modest upgrade, but an upgrade nonetheless. Plus, it is still a solid foldable through and through.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 hands-on review

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 hands-on review

Now on to the actual meat and potatoes of this hands-on experience, as they say. At least we imagine that most people will be interested in our first-hand experience with the new "book-style" foldable?

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 specs at a glance:

  • Body: 161.4x123.9x4.9mm, 201g; IP48 dust and water resistant (dust > 1mm; immersible up to 1.5m for 30 min), Advanced Armor aluminum frame.
  • Display: 7.60" Foldable Dynamic LTPO AMOLED 2X, 120Hz, HDR10+, 1848x2448px resolution, 4:3 aspect ratio, 403ppi; Flex Titanium technology, Cover display:, Dynamic LTPO AMOLED 2X, 120Hz, 5.5 inches, 1248 x 1972 pixels.
  • Chipset: Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy.
  • Memory: 256GB 12GB RAM, 512GB 12GB RAM, 1TB 16GB RAM; UFS 4.x.

  • Rear camera: Wide (main): 50 MP, F/1.8, 1.0µm, Dual Pixel AF PDAF, OIS; Ultra wide angle: 50 MP, F/1.9, 0.7µm, Quad Pixel AF.

  • Video capture: Rear camera: 4K@30/60fps, 1080p@30/60fps, gyro-EIS, OIS; Front camera: 4K@30/60fps, 1080p@30/60fps, gyro-EIS.

  • Connectivity: 5G; eSIM; Wi-Fi 7; BT 6.0, aptX HD; NFC.

Regardless of what we collectively decide to start calling this form factor, there seems to be an undeniable push towards it. The big driving force behind it, of course, is expected to be the upcoming iPhone. The iPhone Ultra, if rumors are anything to go by. It will be Apple's first foldable, and it is expected to offer a similar aspect ratio on its displays. Huawei already has a China-only Pura X Max in this form factor, and we expect other major industry players to follow suit sooner rather than later, definitely on an accelerated timeline once the iPhone becomes a reality. That's just how the wheel usually turns.

One thing potentially worth reiterating before we dig into things is that this is technically not Samsung's "flagship foldable", whatever that combination of words might mean to you. What we mean is that certain expectations must be tempered and we have to be fine with getting an experience that is sort of "almost on the level" of the Z Fold8 Ultra. That means things like no telephoto camera and technically smaller display. Though, they are so different that that point is debatable.

On about the new form factor. It is almost the same size as a passport, which is pretty standardized and the best proverbial "banana for reference" we can offer you right now. We have to say that it quickly grew on us for several reasons, which we will go over. But, almost instantly, we appreciated how easily "pocketable" it is.

It also feels a lot lighter than the Z Fold8 Ultra because it is at just 201 grams. It is technically slightly thicker than the Z Fold8 Ultra, but it's really not noticeably in practice.

Samsung definitely did its best to keep the foldable design as consistent as possible. You get the same flat sides and the same general I/O placement. The displays look boxy here as well, also going against the common industry trend of large rounded corners.

Samsung Galazy Z Fold8

The build seems to be comparable, and although we are yet to get the formal bill of materials confirmation we would ideally want, we believe that the glass on both sides is the same as on the Z Fold8 Ultra and so is the aluminum on the frame. The IP48 ingress protection is certainly shared among Samsung's latest foldable trio.

Before we move on to some actual usability notes, let's look at the color selection real quick. The Z Fold8 is available in the same "standard" Graphite and Cream as the rest of the 2026 Galaxy Z lineup. The hero color is called Lavender, and the Samsung.com exclusive color is Pistachio.

Our demo unit was in Cream, though it didn't seem to have the warm tinge you'd expect to come with the name Cream, and if there is any, it's all but lost in our photos.

Let's talk display, since that is the real novelty factor here. The outside one measures 5.5 inches in diagonal, which is technically smaller than the 6.5-inch panel on the Z Fold8 Ultra, though with a vastly different aspect ratio. The Z Fold8 Ultra has a FullHD+ resolution of 1080 x 2520 pixels on its outside display, which works out to a neat, even if not-exactly-popular 3:7 aspect ratio. It's the definition of "tall and skinny".

In contrast, the Z Fold8 cover display has a resolution of 1,248 x 1,972 pixels, which Samsung calls WUXGA+ and, at first glance, it does seem deceptively higher than the one on the Z Fold8 Ultra, though, in reality, both panels have very similar pixel density figures. The aspect ratio on the Z Fold8 outside display works out to an awkward 312:493, which is quite close to the 10:16 Samsung advertises.

Honestly, it seems very odd at first glance in person. It's not an aspect ratio most of us have really encountered at this size. Though, it arguably makes more practical sense than the "tall and skinny" panel on the Z Fold8 Ultra. Reaching from corner to corner with one hand was not a challenge for us, which is important.

We found usability great here, especially when it came to typing. We definitely found typing on the wide cover display of the Samsung easier than on the Honor Magic 8 Pro. And the iPhone 17 Pro, which we had handy for a side-by-side comparison, only managed to keep up in typing due to its overall superior keyboard layout and programming.

Left: Apple iPhone 17 Pro • Right: Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8

Interestingly, the iPhone 17 Pro did seem to fit more text on a single screen, but the odd aspect on the Z Fold8's cover screen really managed to shine with social media content like Instagram, where we could maximize our screen real estate.

So, we found the cover display to be pretty great in practice. What about the main foldable display? Well, it is a smaller 7.6-inch display with lower resolution than the Z Fold8 Ultra's main display. Though 1,848 x 2,448 pixels is still perfectly sharp, working out to about 403 ppi of density.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 main display productivity

The display on the Z Fold8 is 12 pixels too tall to be a perfect 4:3 ratio. So for all practical intents & purposes, we can round it to a 4:3 aspect ratio when discussing it.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 main display productivity

Anyway, do you enjoy some old-school animated shows? Well, this is about as perfect an experience as you can get. Just us? Well, don't worry, this particular aspect is also a boon for multitasking, with two apps open on either side of the display.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra and Z Fold8 16:9 video playback comparison

We were also kind of interested to know how watching a video in the much more popular 16:9 aspect on the Z Fold8 compared to doing the same on the Z Fold8 Ultra. Well, actually, surprisingly well. Lining the two up as best as possible, the Z Fold8 Ultra does show a slightly larger image, but also much bigger black bars, for what that's worth.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra and Z Fold8 compared

Honestly, we think that this aspect ratio and form factor make more sense overall. Samsung says that both displays are more comfortable and natural regardless of the task, whether it be video watching, gaming or reading. A personal opinion, of course, since it will come down to preference.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 camera viewfinder

Honestly, the one thing that looked really off to us on both screens was the camera viewfinder. Anything outside of Portrait Photo mode just seems awkward, but that's something that you could get used to, we guess.

Other than that, it is perhaps good to note that both panels on the Z Fold8 can refresh at up to 120Hz and seem to both be based on LTPO tech that offers the same dynamic refresh rate switching as on the Z Fold8 Ultra.

Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 minimal display crease

For what it's worth, unlike the Z Fold8 Ultra demo unit we were handed, the Z Fold8 barely had a visible display crease.

Samsung seems to be using very similar tech for the hinges on both phones. The Flex Titanium technology chain we already described in detail in the Z Fold8 Ultra hands-on is here as well. That means an extra flexible titanium-alloy sheet is incorporated inside the display itself and presumably a thinner overall assembly with better durability.

Also, the hinge promises the same improvements to stability and less force required to open the magnetic latch.

While technically the Z Fold8 isn't quite as "feature-packed" as the Z Fold8 Ultra, there are very few actual "shortcomings" to point to. We already mentioned the lack of a dedicated telephoto camera, which is probably the one big omission. Anything past 2x zoom just looked a bit "fuzzy" while shooting from the main 50MP camera. Said 50MP camera appears to be very similar to the one inside the Galaxy S26, which is to say pretty good, but far from industry-leading. Of course, we will reserve any quality judgment for the full review.

The battery inside the Z Fold8 is technically a bit smaller than the Ultra's, rated at 4,800 mAh typical capacity. There is actually still a bit of a debate as of writing this hands-on whether this pack is Si/C like the Ultra's or regular Li-Po.

Regardless, we find it much more meaningful that Samsung included the same 45W charging support on the regular Z Fold8. It promises a 63% charge in around 30 minutes.

As already mentioned, the Z Fold8 uses the same Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 chipset as the Z Fold8 Ultra. Though, based on some of Samsung's wording in the press release, said chip might not be cooled quite as effectively in this form factor. Of course, this is more of a speculation at this point, and we look forward to actually confirming performance and stability in the full review.

All things considered, we very much enjoyed our short initial time with the Z Fold8 and believe it might just have a bright future ahead of it.

Samsung Galaxy Z Flip8 hands-on review

Samsung Galaxy Z Flip8 hands-on review

The Galaxy Z Flip8 finds itself in a pretty hard spot. Not a lot has changed for this year compared to the Z Flip8. We aren't sure what is happening exactly. Perhaps it is hard to meaningfully innovate in this particular form factor. Or maybe Samsung's priorities have shifted. There have been ongoing rumors that this might be the last in the Z Flip line, so maybe the consumer interest just isn't there? We certainly can't say for sure, but what is certain is that Samsung won't be drawing in any new crowds with the sorts of upgrades the Z Flip8 offers.

Design-wise, it is pretty much the same device. The new model is supposedly minimally thinner (like 0.4mm when closed) and something like 8 grams lighter. Other than that - same familiar shape and size.

While it doesn't stand out compared to the rest of the Galaxy Z crowd, we wouldn't exactly say it fits the exact same design mold neatly. The Z Flip8 has generally softer lines with a lot more rounding here and there. Certainly in the corners of both the device and its displays. It is not nearly as "boxy".

While probably purposefully a bit vague about things, it seems that Samsung's quoted upgraded brightness figure of 3,000 nits applies to all three new Galaxy Z devices, including the Z Flip8. So, there's an upgrade for you - around 400 nits more at peak output than last year's model. Still seemingly no LTPO on the cover display, in case anyone was hoping for that in particular.

Also, presumably, Samsung applied similar tuning to the hinge and the magnets to make the opening smoother and easier. We can't say that we noticed it on the Z Flip8 demo unit quite as much as we did on the Z Fold8 Ultra one. So, it's another "take it or leave it" situation.

Other than that - seemingly the same display with the same resolution. Still a noticeable crease on the inside panel.

Samsung Galaxy Z Flip8

The color selection this year includes the Graphite and Cream shared among all the new Galaxy Z family, a Pink hero color and a Samsung.com exclusive Mint color. We got the frankly boring Graphite color on the demo unit.

No battery or charging upgrades here - still the same 4,300 mAh and 25W charging support. Samsung didn't go through the effort to unify charging across the lineup at 45W.

No camera upgrades either: a 50MP main unit and a 12MP ultrawide from what we can tell - the exact same units as last year.

The chipset has been refreshed from the Exynos 2500 to the newer Exynos 2600. We frankly don't expect a massive performance difference as a result, so perhaps we can hope for some battery life improvements from the better manufacturing node and other hardware and software optimizations.

Honestly, the Z Flip8 left us thoroughly underwhelmed. Last year's Z Flip7 will almost certainly be a better and smarter buy at its lower price point.

Software improvements, conclusion

Software improvements

It is hardly a secret that One UI 9 is right around the corner, so it was only logical and perfectly expected that the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra, Z Fold8 and Z Flip8 would launch with One UI 9 and Android 17 underneath.

Samsung didn't particularly highlight anything revolutionary we haven't heard of coming with One UI 9, instead focusing on iterative improvements on things like Now Brief and Now Nudge, promising ongoing privacy and security in an increasingly more agentic AI smartphone environment and also streamlined integration with its new wearables - the Galaxy Watch Ultra2 and Galaxy Watch9 series as well as the upcoming Intelligent Eyewear.

And speaking of agentic AI, as promised, Gemini Intelligence is apparently right around the corner. For anyone not familiar, it promises an almost frightening amount of AI automation and autonomy through the ability to do things like launch and control apps in the background by itself and execute actions on the user's behalf. Through Google and Samsung's ongoing partnership, we fully expect to experience it on Samsung hardware first.

Apparently Samsung SmartSwitch, which has always been one of the better industry solutions for migrating to a new phone, especially from an older Samsung to a newer one, is getting increasingly better with improved support for iOS to Android migration, which sounds intriguing.

In lieu of any meaningful hardware improvements, Samsung did particularly point out a few software ones to look forward to on the Flip8. Apparently there is a new FlexWindow system, which we didn't get to properly test for ourselves yet. As per Samsung's press release, the new FlexWindow "brings apps, insights and actions directly to the cover screen" and also "makes automation through Gemini Intelligence easier to access from the FlexWindow". There is apparently a new Now Brief implementation for FlexWindow as well.

Final thoughts

Overall, Samsung's new crop of Galaxy Z devices turned out to be an intriguing mix of exciting and underwhelming. We definitely found the new Galaxy Z Fold8 form factor very intriguing. There is still a lot of testing to be done and various particulars to be looked into, but on the surface, as far as first impressions go, we kind of like where things are going.

While decidedly less-exciting, the Z Fold8 Ultra does manage to offer some, even if few, upgrades over its predecessor. The battery capacity increase and faster charging are particularly intriguing, but it remains to be seen just how much of a difference they make. The rest is mostly iterative, but it is iterating on an already solid foundation, so we can't complain.

What we can complain about is the new Galaxy Z Flip8. Honestly, it seems that Samsung straight out gave up on this particular model. Whatever the reason behind that decision might be, frankly, the only reason to get excited about the Z Flip8 is that it has the potential to make the Z Flip7 more affordable.
