Introduction and unboxing

Introduction and specs

The Samsung Galaxy Z Fold just went Ultra. With the new addition of the wider and shorter Galaxy Z Fold8 this year, the Z Fold7's successor takes on the Ultra role in the lineup. But is the upgrade limited to the name alone?

The Galaxy Z Fold7 was the first foldable from Samsung that brought significant hardware upgrades in a while, and while the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra doesn't bring nearly as dramatic changes on the outside it still adds a bunch of new stuff inside.

Starting with the expected chip upgrade to Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 (for Galaxy), the new Fold8 Ultra also gains an improved display tech with a less noticeable crease, higher resolution and anti-reflective coating and a better ultrawide camera that brings it closer to the Galaxy S26 Ultra's camera setup. There is also a significantly bigger battery as well as faster wired and wireless charging.

Additionally, Samsung used tougher materials for the frame, without increasing the weight or size of the handset, so it remains just as thin and lightweight as its predecessor.

We are pretty sure the battery and the charging will make a big impact on user experience. The older Folds were stuck at 25W wired charging for far too long, so upping the charging power to 45W is more than welcome. The increase in battery capacity by about 14% (from 4,400 to 5,000 mAh) should put the Z Fold8 Ultra back in the race when it comes to foldables' battery life, too. And Samsung finally embraces the silicon-carbon battery packs, enabling it to improve density and thus save weight.

Some software improvements are also in place - the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra brings some cool new Gemini AI smarts and launches with the latest Android 17 with One UI 9 on top. Yes, that means the Galaxy Z Fold8 and Z Flip8 lineups are the first handsets running Google's latest Android 17 out of the box.

Without further ado, let's see if Samsung still has what it takes to keep up with the growing foldable market.

Unboxing the Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra

The Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra's retail package is business as usual - nothing interesting besides the quick start guides and a USB-C to USB-C cable used for charging and data transfer.

If you are looking to get the fastest possible charging times, consider buying the 45W Samsung charger or a compatible third-party one with the appropriate PPS profile, as the charger isn't included.

Design, build, handling

Design and build

At first glance, the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra looks identical to last year's Z Fold7. There are no major differences to be observed, so it's easy to assume that little changed. However, Samsung has actually revamped the hardware inside, enabling it to make a tougher device with less visible crease and a larger battery in the exact same frame.

One of the first things Samsung boasted about is the thickness. Unfolded, the Z Fold8 Ultra measures just 4.1mm, which puts it on par with the Honor Magic V6 and makes it ever so slightly thinner than the Galaxy Z Fold7 that was 4.2mm. You'd have to look really closely to notice that kind of difference, though.

Samsung is also proud of the new hinge and display design. The new Flex Titanium setup makes the display more durable and uniform while the crease is visibly smaller. Running your fingers through the surface confirms Samsung's claims - the panel does feel more uniform and flat.

Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra

The hinge went through some refinements as well. Inspired by the Galaxy Z TriFold, the Armor FlexHinge requires less pressure to be opened and is assisted by magnets, making the operation more effortless and smoother.

The rest of the chassis is encased in Armor Aluminum alloy that Samsung uses for its Galaxy S lineup and previous Fold generations, while the glass panel covering the outer screen has been upgraded to Gorilla Glass Victus Ceramic 3. The back is protected by Gorilla Glass Victus 2. The usual IP48 protection against water and dust is in place.

Despite all that the weight is unchanged at 215 grams. And let's not forget we've got a bigger 5,000 mAh battery included in that.

There's nothing out of the ordinary when going around the sides - the fingerprint reader sits inside the power button on the right, while the SIM card tray is located near the speaker grille on top of the left half. You get a single SIM card slot this time around, so you can insert only one physical nano SIM. That means your second SIM must be an eSIM. You can also opt for two eSIMs, of course.

Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra

The available color options are only three this time - Graphite, Cream and Violet Shadow - we have the latter in our hands. There's also a Green Shadow version, but it's exclusive to Samsung's official web store and physical Samsung Experience stores.

After last year's Fold introduced a major redesign we never expected the Z Fold8 Ultra to look drastically different. Yet we are pleasantly surprised by the number of refinements Samsung brought. The hinge is nicer and smoother, and the display has a less noticeable crease. Sturdier and longer lasting without being heavier is also a big win.

Lab tests - display, battery life, charging speed, speakers

New Flex Titanium 8.0-inch OLED and the good old 6.5-inch cover screen

Although there's no change to the internal screen size and it still sits at 8 inches, the flexible 120Hz LTPO OLED panel has been upgraded in several ways. For starters, the resolution has been bumped up to 2256 x 2504 pixels. HDR10+ support is still at hand, but unfortunately, still no Dolby Vision.

Samsung is also advertising the display as brighter than before, but perhaps the most important updates are the so-called Flex Titanium technology and the anti-reflective coating. The latter has been around for three Galaxy S26 Ultra generations now, and it improves sunlight legibility quite a bit.

Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra vs. Galaxy Z Fold8 vs. Galaxy S26 Ultra

The Flex Titanium tech, on the other hand, improves usability differently. The new display module incorporates a thin titanium alloy sheet sitting under the OLED panel and a separate titanium plate supporting the entire display module. These titanium pieces improve durability and uniformity. The crease is less prominent not just visibly, but to the touch as well.

It remains to be seen whether the crease will become bigger with time, as is usually the case with the previous foldable panels, but practice shows you stop noticing it pretty quickly so it probably doesn't matter.

When it comes to the cover display, there are no apparent changes, nor does Samsung advertise them. We are dealing with the same 6.5-inch LTPO OLED panel with 120Hz refresh rate and 1080 x 2520 pixel resolution.

Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra cover screen

We didn't see an increase in peak brightness on a 75% white coverage, but we do see an improvement in the 10% patch test where the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra reached almost 3,000 nits, as advertised. The maximum auto brightness at 75% is still around 1,400 nits, but due to the anti-reflective coating, sunlight legibility is better.

Refresh rate

The refresh rate control is pretty straightforward. Samsung offers two modes - Adaptive and Standard. The standard caps the refresh rate to 60Hz, but it's still adapting to the content displayed.

The displays can go down to 24Hz to preserve power when the system deems necessary or when you are playing a 24fps video on your phone. It works on YouTube. And since both the inner and outer panels are LTPO, the system dials down to 1Hz when the display is idle.

High refresh gaming is supported as long as the game title offers higher frame rate rendering.

Battery life

The Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra offers a significant battery upgrade over the Galaxy Z Fold7, even if the battery is not the largest by today's standards. Most handsets that moved from regular Li-Po batteries to Si/C cells boast bigger jumps in capacity. Still, the bump in battery capacity from 4,400 mAh to 5,000 mAh is more than welcome.

The battery tests below show a notable jump not just in the overall Active Use Score, but also in screen-on tests like web browsing and video streaming. On the other hand, the Z Fold8 Ultra still lags behind some of its competitors with larger batteries in the 6,000+ mAh range. The vanilla Z Fold8 posts similar runtimes with a lesser battery, but with a smaller screen that likely compensates.

The same goes for the cover screen. You do get a bit of extra runtime on the screen-on tests, but still behind most competitors in this regard.

Charging

Samsung phones, and foldables in particular, have never been among the fastest to charge. But with this year's Galaxy S and Galaxy Z Fold generations, the smartphone maker is making notable changes in this regard. The Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra now supports 45W wired charging over Power Delivery with the appropriate PPS profile, up from 25W. The wireless charging has also been bumped up to 25W from 15W.

That may not sound like much on paper, but our tests show that the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra is finally competitive. The total charging time from 1% to 100% has been cut by about half an hour, while a 30-minute charging cycle gives you 71% of the charge back, a notable improvement compared to the 53% charge that the Fold7 offered within the same time.

Interestingly, there are a handful of foldables that beat the Samsung to the finish line, yet the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra is quicker than most in the first 15 and 30 minutes of our charging test. Arguably, Samsung's charging curve makes more sense, since nine out of ten times when you need quick top-ups is when your battery is approaching 0%, so making the curve steeper in the first half an hour of the test makes a bigger difference in the real world.

Speakers

The Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra features a full-fledged stereo speaker setup this time around, or at least the top speaker behind the earpiece gains another slit. On paper, this should improve sound quality and perhaps loudness too, because in the previous models, the sound was firing only from the cover screen's earpiece at the back (when using the phone's inner screen).

Speaker grilles

However, our sound tests seem to suggest otherwise. The -29.3 LUFS loudness score is about "Average" and a tad below last year's Fold7. It's also quieter than most competitors. But that's not the main issue here. Tuning has been modified as well.

While music tracks sound fuller and deeper, the bass seems to overpower the mids and vocals. Everything sounds somewhat muffled. We like the old tuning better - the Z Fold7 sounds cleaner and louder.

Use the Playback controls to listen to the phone sample recordings (best use headphones). We measure the average loudness of the speakers in LUFS. A lower absolute value means a louder sound. A look at the frequency response chart will tell you how far off the ideal "0db" flat line is the reproduction of the bass, treble, and mid frequencies. You can add more phones to compare their differences. The scores and ratings are not comparable with our older loudspeaker test. Learn more about how we test here.

Software and performance

One UI 9.0 based on Android 17

The Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra boots the latest Android 17 out of the box, garnished with Samsung's own take on the Android experience - One UI 9.0. You can expect 7 major OS updates and security patches - the same excellent commitment Samsung flagships have enjoyed for a while now. This is the most generous update policy in the Android world besides Google.

Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra vs. Z Fold8

One UI 9.0 continues the trend that One UI 6.1 started a while ago by heavily focusing on AI features. But let's get the obvious and not-so-obvious bits out of the way first.

Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra multi-tasking

One UI is arguably the best software experience you can get on an Android phone. Split-screen features, UI elements and general foldable-related features are what make One UI so good. This year, for example, Samsung subtly changed the behavior of the screen rotation. For instance, if you were using an app in landscape orientation on your inner screen, switching to the cover screen will transfer the app in the same orientation and will remain locked that way at first.

Home screen • Recent apps

Additionally, the Now Nudge feature instantly switches to split-screen view when you, for example, decide to take action on a suggestion to add a calendar event. The calendar will appear on the other half of the screen, so you don't have to switch between apps.

Settings menu and Gemini AI

Small, but neat software tweaks make the foldable One UI experience much nicer.

When it comes to design and overall experience, there are no drastic changes compared to One UI 8.5 that debuted with the Galaxy S series. You can check out our comprehensive One UI 8.5 video review below for more context.

Starting with the overall UI, it all looks very familiar, but just a bit "tidier" and "cleaned up" here and there. For example, the brightness and volume sliders in the quick actions interface (which is now completely separate, by the way) appear to be a bit thicker and easier to use by default. They are also quite customizabl.

Notification shade • Quick toggles and controls

You might remember how the Galaxy AI menu had extensive text descriptions attached to every item, which made things a bit overwhelming at first glance. Those are now gone, with any additional text relegated to the bottom of the menu. It's small touches of polish like that.

An entirely new addition we noticed is the Warranty and Care menu in settings. It is very convenient. What it does is gather all of the Samsung devices logged under your account into one hub. From here you can view some info, including warranty and quickly try to fix issues and troubleshoot things.

Warranty and service menu

Now let's talk Galaxy AI. Call Assist isn't new, but it only used to have Call screening and Live translate. Now there is a Call brief option. If you opt in, it listens to your calls carefully, and if it picks up on any information it potentially has relevant details on, like you start discussing an event that happens to be in your calendar, it will automatically pop up the info right in your active call screen.


Call briefing

Speaking of "briefings", Samsung's Now Brief - the already highly-customizable feed has gotten even more personal with the addition of custom cards. These are AI-powered, which is probably why the system currently limits you to only three at a time, and there are also some limitations on how often you can create and edit prompts for the cards.

Now Brief customizable cards

Currently, the feature has its "sources" organized in three categories - Weather, Health and Videos. These broadly determine where the data will be pulled from. Some sources might require additional permissions to communicate with third-party apps like YouTube. We were able to very effortlessly create a tailored feed of GSMArena YouTube videos only related to Samsung. It's a pretty good idea, especially if it grows in scope.

Bixby, now featuring with Perplexity access, has new widgets. These are mostly meant to offer quick access to voice prompts as well as chat history and act a lot like Gemini's similar interfaces.

Driving report seems to be something new we haven't seen before. It is curated in the Connected devices menu. What it promises is to log all of your car trips and various telematics automatically. All you have to do is select your car's Bluetooth connection so it can identify it and, of course, give the app a lot of permissions.


Driving report

My FanCam is a new addition to the Galaxy AI roster. It is an AI-backed auto-framing and zooming video editor. It works on any video (seems to require HEVC encoding), not just the ones captured by the phone, which is great. You just select a person (it has to be a person as far as we can tell) and then the AI analyzes the following video frames and re-crops things so that the person stays in frame. It is even smart enough to recognize events like a ball being thrown and going inside a hoop so it can follow that action as well. It works surprisingly well.

Back to Now Nudge again, it can now be found in more scenarios than just your keyboard while typing. You will find it hovering over your chat in the bottom-right corner or offering quick access to an app right from the notification card.


Now Nudge

Advanced Automation helps you do stuff without leaving the app you are using. Samsung calls it that because you can simply ask the AI to look for relevant bookings while looking at a nice photo on Instagram. Gemini AI will understand which place on the screen is being shown and will suggest bookings for that area.

Gemini Notebook

Gemini Notebook is just a clever way to leverage AI for taking notes. These notes, as a result, can be more complex. You can add sources like a photo, news reports, documents, voice notes, presentations, etc., and Gemini will sort them for you and answer questions based on the notes in your Notebook. You can even ask it to make relevant infographics. We have a strong suspicion that this feature will come to other Android 17 devices in the near future.

Benchmark performance

Like last year, the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra doesn't run on the regular flagship SoC from Qualcomm. It employs an overclocked version of the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5, and Samsung calls it SD 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy. The Galaxy S26 Ultra (and the S26 and S26+ in some regions) were the first and only phones to launch with the chipset, but that exclusivity has ended. The SM8850-1-AD SoC can now be found in other phones as well.

In any case, the  "AD" suffix indicates that the main 2x Oryon V3 Phoenix L cores are clocked at 4.74 GHz instead of 4.6 GHz. The rest of the 6x Oryon V3 Phoenix M cores remain at 3.62 GHz. The Adreno 840 GPU is also supposedly clocked higher. The AD version of the SoC should have its Adreno 840 clocked at 1300 MHz, as opposed to 1200 MHz on the vanilla AC version.

The available memory configurations are 12GB/256GB, 12GB/512GB, 16GB/512GB and 16GB/1TB. As evident from the scores Samsung is using very fast UFS storage, even if it doesn't confirm if they are UFS 4.0 or UFS 4.1.

Camera - photo and video quality

Updated ultrawide, otherwise the Z Fold7's setup

The Z Fold8 Ultra inherits the camera hardware from the Z Fold7, with a small update to the ultrawide camera - it's more or less the same story as the transition from the S24 Ultra to the S25 Ultra. That means a triple rear setup headlined by a 200MP main camera, and two 10MP cameras for selfies, one per display.

The main camera is based on the HP2 sensor, same as last year, and same as that on the S26 Ultra - Samsung's best, so to speak. The Folds' space constraints mean a somewhat dimmer lens - the aperture is f/1.7 here, same as on the Z Fold7, while the S26 Ultra's value is f/1.4.

As we mused in the review of the Z Fold8 (non-Ultra), the ultrawide camera is probably using a Sony IMX 858 imager - it's got the right specs and it's on the list of sensors that the phone reports (which contains a lot of bits that are clearly not to be found on board the Z Fold8 Ultra). The S26 Ultra and the S25 Ultra before it had an in-house JN3 sensor of essentially the same specs.

The telephoto reports the same 3K1 sensor we saw on the Z Fold7, which we saw on the S26 (but not the S26+ or the S26 Ultra). It's a smallish 10MP imager but you're still going to be getting 12MP photos.

The two selfie cameras are also specced at 10MP, but they're more honest and save 10MP files. We can't name the sensors (as Samsung isn't either), but we're fairly confident they're the same as on the Z Fold7. The two cameras still have different focal lengths - 23mm on the cover and a much wider 17mm on the internal one (as per the EXIF, at least; otherwise the field of view that Samsung quotes corresponds more closely to 24mm and 18mm).

Video quality

The Z Fold8 Ultra supports the full set of video recording capabilities that Samsung has to offer - Pro mode, codecs, resolutions and frame rates, it's all there.

In the regular Video mode, the phone can record up to 4K60 with all five of its cameras (including the two 'selfie' cameras), with the main and ultrawide rear cameras also supporting 8K30.

The Pro video mode adds a wide variety of other frame rates, including 24, 25, 48, 50, 100, and 120fps modes for 4K (the 100 and 120fps are only for the main and ultrawide cameras), and 24fps for 8K.

The out-of-the-box setting for the video codec is h.265 (HEVC), but you can opt for the older and less efficient h.264 codec. The Folds also get the Samsung-developed pro-grade APV codec (Advanced Professional Video) - the S26 Ultra had that, but not the plain S26s. It enables high-quality perceptually lossless encoding resulting in substantial file sizes (1.5GB for a minute of 1080p30), which is why Samsung recommends recording to external media in this case.

There's an option for 10-bit HDR10+ recording, as well as a Log option that can be enabled for both Pro video and regular Video mode, complete with a selection of pre-loaded LUTs (Standard, Blockbuster, Coming-of-age, Romance and Thriller). In Pro mode you also get finer control over focus and exposure (complete with zebras and false color aids), plus mic directionality settings and audio level indicators.

Video stabilization is available in all modes on all cameras, and you can disable it in settings if you have other means of keeping the phone steady. The Horizon lock stabilization mode is also available on the Z Fold8 Ultra, without any of the fuss surrounding the feature's announcement alongside the S26 series.

Alternatives, pros and cons, verdict

Competition

The Galaxy Z Fold series has been creeping up the price ladder for the last few years, and the new Z Fold8 Ultra got its corresponding "ultra" price. The handset starts at $2,100/€2,200/INR 200,000 in for the base 12GB/256GB memory configuration.

Verdict

Is the pioneer of foldable phones waking up from its slumber? It definitely looks like it. The Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra offers a big upgrade over its predecessor with higher-quality inner screen with a smaller crease and anti-reflective coating, a larger battery, faster charging, a new ultrawide cam. There's also a host of new software features that further refine the foldable experience in general.

On the other hand, the few years of stagnation mean Samsung is still playing catch-up with the rest of the competition, which overtook it in terms of camera, battery life and came close to matching the UI experience. And until the price settles, the Z Fold8 Ultra remains drastically more expensive.

Design, build, handling

Design and build

At first glance, the Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra looks identical to last year's Z Fold7. There are no major differences to be observed, so it's easy to assume that little changed. However, Samsung has actually revamped the hardware inside, enabling it to make a tougher device with less visible crease and a larger battery in the exact same frame.

One of the first things Samsung boasted about is the thickness. Unfolded, the Z Fold8 Ultra measures just 4.1mm, which puts it on par with the Honor Magic V6 and makes it ever so slightly thinner than the Galaxy Z Fold7 that was 4.2mm. You'd have to look really closely to notice that kind of difference, though.

Samsung is also proud of the new hinge and display design. The new Flex Titanium setup makes the display more durable and uniform while the crease is visibly smaller. Running your fingers through the surface confirms Samsung's claims - the panel does feel more uniform and flat.

Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra

The hinge went through some refinements as well. Inspired by the Galaxy Z TriFold, the Armor FlexHinge requires less pressure to be opened and is assisted by magnets, making the operation more effortless and smoother.

The rest of the chassis is encased in Armor Aluminum alloy that Samsung uses for its Galaxy S lineup and previous Fold generations, while the glass panel covering the outer screen has been upgraded to Gorilla Glass Victus Ceramic 3. Refresh rate

The refresh rate control is pretty straightforward. Samsung offers two modes - Adaptive and Standard. The standard caps the refresh rate to 60Hz, but it's still adapting to the content displayed.

The displays can go down to 24Hz to preserve power when the system deems necessary or when you are playing a 24fps video on your phone. It works on YouTube. And since both the inner and outer panels are LTPO, the system dials down to 1Hz when the display is idle.

High refresh gaming is supported as long as the game title offers higher frame rate rendering.

Samsung phones, and foldables in particular, have never been among the fastest to charge. The Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra features a full-fledged stereo speaker setup this time around, or at least the top speaker behind the earpiece gains another slit. Speaker grilles

However, our sound tests seem to suggest otherwise. The -29.3 LUFS loudness score is about "Average" and a tad below last year's Fold7. It's also quieter than most competitors. But that's not the main issue here. Tuning has been modified as well.

Camera - photo and video quality

Updated ultrawide, otherwise the Z Fold7's setup

The Z Fold8 Ultra inherits the camera hardware from the Z Fold7, with a small update to the ultrawide camera - it's more or less the same story as the transition from the S24 Ultra to the S25 Ultra. That means a triple rear setup headlined by a 200MP main camera, and two 10MP cameras for selfies, one per display.

The main camera is based on the HP2 sensor, same as last year, and same as that on the S26 Ultra - Samsung's best, so to speak. The Folds' space constraints mean a somewhat dimmer lens - the aperture is f/1.7 here, same as on the Z Fold7, while the S26 Ultra's value is f/1.4.

As we mused in the review of the Z Fold8 (non-Ultra), the ultrawide camera is probably using a Sony IMX 858 imager - it's got the right specs and it's on the list of sensors that the phone reports (which contains a lot of bits that are clearly not to be found on board the Z Fold8 Ultra). The S26 Ultra and the S25 Ultra before it had an in-house JN3 sensor of essentially the same specs.

The telephoto reports the same 3K1 sensor we saw on the Z Fold7, which we saw on the S26 (but not the S26+ or the S26 Ultra). It's a smallish 10MP imager but you're still going to be getting 12MP photos.

The two selfie cameras are also specced at 10MP, but they're more honest and save 10MP files. We can't name the sensors (as Samsung isn't either), but we're fairly confident they're the same as on the Z Fold7. The two cameras still have different focal lengths - 23mm on the cover and a much wider 17mm on the internal one (as per the EXIF, at least; otherwise the field of view that Samsung quotes corresponds more closely to 24mm and 18mm).

Video quality

The Z Fold8 Ultra supports the full set of video recording capabilities that Samsung has to offer - Pro mode, codecs, resolutions and frame rates, it's all there.

In the regular Video mode, the phone can record up to 4K60 with all five of its cameras (including the two 'selfie' cameras), with the main and ultrawide rear cameras also supporting 8K30.

The Pro video mode adds a wide variety of other frame rates, including 24, 25, 48, 50, 100, and 120fps modes for 4K (the 100 and 120fps are only for the main and ultrawide cameras), and 24fps for 8K.

The out-of-the-box setting for the video codec is h.265 (HEVC), but you can opt for the older and less efficient h.264 codec. The Folds also get the Samsung-developed pro-grade APV codec (Advanced Professional Video) - the S26 Ultra had that, but not the plain S26s. It enables high-quality perceptually lossless encoding resulting in substantial file sizes (1.5GB for a minute of 1080p30), which is why Samsung recommends recording to external media in this case.

There's an option for 10-bit HDR10+ recording, as well as a Log option that can be enabled for both Pro video and regular Video mode, complete with a selection of pre-loaded LUTs (Standard, Blockbuster, Coming-of-age, Romance and Thriller). In Pro mode you also get finer control over focus and exposure (complete with zebras and false color aids), plus mic directionality settings and audio level indicators.

Video stabilization is available in all modes on all cameras, and you can disable it in settings if you have other means of keeping the phone steady. The Horizon lock stabilization mode is also available on the Z Fold8 Ultra, without any of the fuss surrounding the feature's announcement alongside the S26 series.

Alternatives, pros and cons, verdict

Competition

The Galaxy Z Fold series has been creeping up the price ladder for the last few years, and the new Z Fold8 Ultra got its corresponding "ultra" price. The handset starts at $2,100/€2,200/INR 200,000 in for the base 12GB/256GB memory configuration.

This means Z Fold8 Ultra's main competitors undercut it by around 20-30%, although the record-breaking Z Fold8 Ultra pre-orders suggests many people just don't care.

But what about the hardware? Despite Samsung's decent haul of upgrades this year, the competition still seems to have an edge here slacking. In fact, we have a new player in town - the Motorola Razr Fold, which snaps better stills than the Z Fold8 Ultra and outlasts it in our battery tests. It's also around €600/$200 cheaper with twice the storage, too.

Perhaps the biggest threat remains the Honor Magic V6 - it has the widest market reach of the rivals (if still far from Samsung's), but offers a better camera experience, larger battery and arguably even durable chassis. It has everything the Z Fold8 Ultra has but sits at a much lower €1,700.

Motorola Razr Fold • Honor Magic V6 • vivo X Fold6

And while we are waiting on the vivo X Fold5 successor in India, you might want to check out the current X Fold generation as it gives the Z Fold8 Ultra a run for its money - longer battery life and slightly faster charging (with caveats). If the current X Fold5 is any indication, the X Fold6 will be a serious threat to the Z Fold8 Ultra in India with improved camera hardware as a bonus.

Back to the global stage, the Oppo Find N6 makes a very compelling argument by beating the Z Fold8 Ultra in our battery and charging tests and delivering an overall better camera experience and more feature-rich and slightly larger displays with Dolby Vision and stylus support. And once again, it's cheaper, too.

Oppo Find N6 • Samsung Galaxy Z Fold8 • Samsung Galaxy Z Fold7

What about the vanilla Galaxy Z Fold8? This one boils down to form factor and cameras. You will be sacrificing the telephoto camera and main camera sensor for arguably a better, more manageable body. The shorter and wider screens do serve just as well for multimedia consumption and gaming, though productivity is slightly compromised. And the vanilla Fold8 is €200/$200 cheaper, let's not forget.

Galaxy Z fold8 Ultra vs. Z Fold8

Lastly, you might want to consider yesteryear's Z Fold7, which is now €900 cheaper and gets you a lot of the way. Sure, you won't get the battery and charging improvements, nor the nicer and brighter foldable screen with less prominent crease, but at this point in time, the huge price gap is hardly justifiable.

Verdict

Is the pioneer of foldable phones waking up from its slumber? It definitely looks like it. The Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra offers a big upgrade over its predecessor with higher-quality inner screen with a smaller crease and anti-reflective coating, a larger battery, faster charging, a new ultrawide cam. There's also a host of new software features that further refine the foldable experience in general.

On the other hand, the few years of stagnation mean Samsung is still playing catch-up with the rest of the competition, which overtook it in terms of camera, battery life and came close to matching the UI experience. And until the price settles, the Z Fold8 Ultra remains drastically more expensive.
