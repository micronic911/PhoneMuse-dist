Introduction, specs, unboxing

Introduction

Today, we are looking at the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G - yes, the same phone that launched 7 months ago. Somehow this review managed to spend the last five months hiding in our drafts folder. We've finally tracked it down, dusted it off, and it's time to right that wrong. Turns out reviews don't have expiration dates. Better late than never.

To make the distinction, we are looking specifically at the 4G version of the Redmi Note 15 Pro, which should not be confused with the Redmi Note 15 Pro 5G. Although the two look somewhat similar and share a few specs, they belong to entirely different market segments.

The Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G clearly sits a step below its 5G sibling in almost every hardware way. As the name suggests, it is a 4G-only device and one equipped with a rather meager MediaTek Helio G200 Ultra chipset - probably the weakest link in the spec sheet.

Xiaomi Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G specs at a glance:

  • Body: 163.2x76.3x8.0mm, 195g; Glass front (Gorilla Glass Victus 2), plastic back; IP65 dust-tight and water-resistant (low-pressure water jets).
  • Display: 6.77" AMOLED, 68B colors, 120Hz, 3840Hz PWM, 3200 nits (peak), 1080x2392px resolution, 19.93:9 aspect ratio, 388ppi.
  • Chipset: Mediatek Helio G200 Ultra (6 nm).
  • Memory: 256GB 8GB RAM, 256GB 12GB RAM, 512GB 12GB RAM; UFS 2.2; microSDXC (uses shared SIM slot).
  • OS/Software: Android 15, up to 4 major Android upgrades, HyperOS 2.
  • Rear camera: Wide (main): 200 MP, f/1.7, 23mm, 1/1.4", 0.56µm, multi-directional PDAF, OIS; Ultra wide angle: 8 MP, f/2.2, 15mm, 120-degree, 1/4.0", 1.12µm.
  • Front camera: 32 MP, f/2.2, (wide), 1/3.0", 0.7µm.
  • Video capture: Rear camera: 1080p@30/60fps, gyro-EIS; Front camera: 1080p@30fps.
  • Battery: 6500mAh; 45W wired, 18W reverse wired.
  • Connectivity: LTE; Wi-Fi 5; BT 5.3; NFC; Infrared port.
  • Misc: Fingerprint reader (under display, optical); stereo speakers (with Dolby Atmos).

The Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G also comes with some notable downgrades in durability compared to the 5G version. Not that IP65 certification is bad or anything, but the phone just isn't quite as tough as its sibling.

Other than that, Xiaomi mostly made sure to achieve spec parity, or come close to that, for most other aspects of the 4G model. The display, while not a direct match, is still a 12-bit AMOLED with a 120Hz refresh rate. You do have to live with a slightly smaller diagonal, lower resolution and the lack of HDR support, though.

The batteries on the two phones are practically identical, and it is great to see that the lower-end 4G model also gets a Si/C pack, as well as the same 45W fast charging. The camera setup also looks very similar across the pair, at least on paper. There is the important and unfortunate caveat here that video capture is limited to 1080p due to chipset deficiencies.

The Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G does have one small but potentially quite significant thing going for it that the 5G model lacks - a microSD card slot for storage expansion.

Unboxing

The Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G ships in a rather unassuming two-piece cardboard box. It is quite thick and provides plenty of protection for shipping. The color scheme of choice is red and white, which is quite eye-catching. There is only cardboard on the inside of the box, which is quite eco-friendly.

The accessory package is quite lavish, especially by modern standards. You get a soft and pretty thick gray TPU case in the box. Of course, there is a USB Type-A to Type-C cable. Along with it, Xiaomi also throws in a 45W fast charger. It should, however, be noted that due to EU regulations, those markets do not get a charger in the box. Another important note to make - both the charger and the cable are proprietary, and you should keep track of both in order to charge at full speed.

Design, build quality, handling

Design, build quality, handling

We wouldn't exactly call the Redmi Note 15 Pro's design language iconic or even quite memorable, but it is at least sufficiently distinct. The large camera island is a signature touch. It is color-matched with the rest of the back side, which is also a nice touch. The camera island is also shared across the Redmi Note 15 Pro 5G and 4G.

Since we are on the topic of the phone's back side, it is made of plastic like the Redmi Note 15 Pro 5G. The finish is quite smooth and almost velvety to the touch.

The Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G is available in Black, Glacier Blue and Titanium. All of the colors are pretty subdued and would fit nicely in any environment, even a boardroom.

Unlike the Redmi Note 15 Pro 5G, its 4G variant is quite a bit curvier. Both its front and back feature curved edges. This arguably makes for a more comfortable in-hand fit, though in practice the difference isn't that major.

Unfortunately, if you plan on applying a glass screen protector to your Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G, you will run into some issues related to the curved edges.

The phone feels very solid and well-made. It has absolutely no flex or hollowness. You also get IP65 ingress protection. While not quite as impressive as the IP68/IP69K rating of the Redmi Note 15 Pro 5G, this is still good enough for most people and day-to-day situations.

The bill of materials is actually quite good. There is a plastic frame, but it gives off a convincing aluminum effect.

Xiaomi Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G

The rest of the materials include plastic for the back side and Corning Gorilla Glass Victus 2 for the front, just like on the Redmi Note 15 Pro 5G.

The control layout is quite standard. There is a power button on the right side, along with a volume rocker. Both are clicky and offer nice tactile feedback. The left-hand side is empty. There are speaker grills on both the top and the bottom of the device for a better and more balanced audio output. The top frame also houses the secondary noise-canceling mic and an IR blaster. The bottom side has the main mic, the Type-C port and a SIM tray. That SIM tray houses either two Nano-SIM cards or, alternatively, you could swap one of the SIMs for a MicroSD storage card.

There are no visible sensors anywhere on the front of the phone. Everything is neatly hidden away underneath the display.

The fingerprint reader is also underneath the panel. It is a simple optical unit, well-paced and quite reliable and speedy in its operation. We have no complaints about its accuracy or operation.

Our lab tests - display, battery life, charging speed, speaker

Display

Xiaomi has a great track record when it comes to delivering quality panels even on its lower-end devices. The Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G is no exception. The display here is definitely slightly less impressive than on the Redmi Note 15 Pro 5G. You get slightly lower resolution and no HDR support.

Even so, the 6.77-inch OLED display looks very impressive in person. It is spacious and quite sharp thanks to its 388 ppi and its 1080 x 2392-pixel resolution. The display also offers a 120Hz refresh rate and 12-bit color reproduction.

Xiaomi markets an impressive 3,200 nits of peak brightness for the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G, just like on the 5G version. We did our standardized testing and indeed recorded some very impressive brightness numbers. In fact, slightly better numbers than even the 5G model.

The minimum brightness we measured at point white was 2.1 nits.

As mentioned, the phone has a 120Hz refresh rate. It behaves in the exact same way as the 5G model. There are two refresh rate modes to choose from. The standard one automatically adapts the refresh rate based on what is happening on screen and what the current application supports. The phone can switch between 30Hz, 60Hz, 90Hz and 120Hz operation. Since this is an LTPS panel, it can't really do any advanced adaptive switching, just fixed steps.

Alternatively, you can use the "Custom" refresh rate toggle and either lock the phone to 60Hz or select which apps you would like to run at up to 120Hz on a per-app basis. It's a very flexible setup.

As mentioned, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G lacks HDR support. Interestingly, the phone doesn't report any HDR video decoding capabilities either.

Fortunately, the phone does support the highest possible Google Widevine L1 DRM, allowing streaming services like Netflix to offer up high-resolution content and saturate its display resolution.

Battery life

The Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G has a large 6,500 mAh battery, which is practically the same capacity as the 5G model. Modern Si/C chemistry allows for better density. The MediaTek Helio G200 Ultra practically sips power for most tasks. It excels in call endurance and video streaming. Web browsing and gaming could be a bit better, but are still good in the grand scheme of things.

Charging speed

Just like its 5G sibling, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G advertised 45W fast charging. It is done through a proprietary Xiaomi adapter with a proprietary USB Type-A to Type-C cable.

We measured actual charging speeds and weren't overly impressed. Still, a full charge in about an hour and 20 minutes is not too bad. In any case, despite sharing the same official 45W charging rating, the Redmi Note 14 Pro 4G definitely charges a bit slower than its 5G sibling.

As we said, some markets get the 45W Xiaomi charger free in the box, but don't expect to get it in the EU markets.

Beyond the regular wired charging, the phone does offer reverse wired charging at up to 18W, compared to 22.5W on the 5G model. There is no wireless charging.

The Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G has a few battery protection options when it comes to charging. Mainly, you can set a charge limit, either a fixed one at 80% or a "Smart Charging" mode that promises to learn your usage patterns and charge to 80% when appropriate.

Speakers - loudness and quality

The Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G comes equipped with a stereo speaker setup, consisting of a bottom-firing main unit and a secondary top-firing speaker. The two don't appear to be perfectly matched in size, and that asymmetry is noticeable in real-world use.

That said, overall balance is quite respectable. The phone delivers a reasonably wide soundstage with pleasingly rich mids, making vocals and dialogue sound clear and natural. As expected for a device of this class, bass is essentially absent, and at higher volume levels, the treble becomes overly aggressive, introducing audible distortion.

It seems Xiaomi has prioritized sheer loudness over absolute sound fidelity. The volume slider can be pushed all the way up to a rather extreme 400%, but for clean and comfortable listening, keeping it at or below the standard 100% level is strongly recommended. While the speakers do get impressively loud at maximum output, the resulting sound is far from enjoyable.

On the software side, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G offers Dolby Atmos support, complete with a graphic equalizer and several preset profiles. Users can also opt for Xiaomi's own audio tuning solution, providing a bit of flexibility when tailoring the sound to personal preferences.

Use the Playback controls to listen to the phone sample recordings (best use headphones). We measure the average loudness of the speakers in LUFS. A lower absolute value means a louder sound. A look at the frequency response chart will tell you how far off the ideal "0db" flat line is the reproduction of the bass, treble, and mid frequencies. You can add more phones to compare how they differ. The scores and ratings are not comparable with our older loudspeaker test. Learn more about how we test here.

Connectivity

As its name suggests, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G is a 4G-only device. At least you get dual SIM support. This includes two physical Nano-SIM cards. As per our testing, there is no eSIM support, but that might be market-dependent. Check with your local retailer.

Positioning includes GPS, GALILEO, GLONASS, QZSS and BDS support. For local connectivity, the phone has dual-band Wi-Fi AC/5, which is a step down from what the 5G model offers. The same goes for the Bluetooth 5.3 support, instead of 5.4. At least you still get Bluetooth LE. Some markets get NFC as well, but you have to check with your retailer of choice for that. There is no 3.5 mm audio jack and no FM radio receiver. You do, however, get an IR blaster located on the top frame.

The Type-C port is backed up by a basic USB 2.0 data connection, which means a maximum theoretical data throughput of 480 Mbps. USB OTG/Host Mode is supported, but there are no really fancy features like video output over USB Alt mode.

For sensors, you get an ST lsm6dsvqtr accelerometer and gyroscope combo, a QST qmc6308 magnetometer and compass combo, an SI sip1328 light sensor and what is unfortunately a virtual Elliptic Labs proximity sensor. It works reliably enough for turning the display off during calls, which is at least a positive.

Software and performance

HyperOS is moving forward

When we reviewed it at the beginning of the year, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G shipped with HyperOS 2 on top of Android 15. Due to the unforeseen delay of this review, the phone has already received an update to HyperOS 3.1 and Android 16, so our review draft was a bit outdated in this aspect, and this section is shorter than usual.

As far as the manufacturer's software update policy goes, Xiaomi has announced that the Redmi Note 15 series will receive 4 major OS updates and 6 years of security patches, which is in line with the most up-to-date software policy for the Redmi Note 15 family.

Benchmarks and performance

We have arguably arrived at the Achilles' heel of the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G. The MediaTek Helio G200 Ultra is simply put underwhelming in terms of performance, no two ways about it. The G200 is essentially the G100, which in turn is essentially the G99, and none of these is impressive in any manner whatsoever.

Despite being a 2025 model, as we said, the G200 Ultra is essentially a rebranded entry-level chipset from 2022. While its 6nm TSMC manufacturing node is modern enough by current standards, the actual hardware here is pretty bad. The CPU setup consists of a retro two-plus-four core configuration, with two Cortex-A76 units, working at up to 2.2 GHz and another six Cortex-A55 units, clocked at up to 2.0 GHz. In case you were wondering, the Cortex-A55 was launched in 2017.

Things don't get better in the GPU department, where the G200 Ultra gets a slightly overclocked Mali-G57 MC2, working at up to 1.1 GHz. Again, a pretty aging part that was never meant to be impressive to begin with and one that has really started showing its cracks.

Credit where credit is due, the Helio G100 and G200 do introduce a slightly upgraded ISP that handles up to a 200MP single camera sensor, for what that's worth, since video capture is still capped at 1080p. Major disappointment there.

Actual benchmark results only put the disappointment into perspective. You are essentially getting the same lackluster performance as last year's Redmi Note 14 Pro 4G, and it's not like HyperOS has gotten any lighter in that time. The closest comparable chipset here is the Snapdragon 6s Gen 3, and it's not a shining beacon of performance either.

The global Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G model can be had in any of the following memory configurations: 256GB/8GB, 256GB/12GB and 512GB/12GB. All of the tiers include dual-channel LPDDR4X RAM and UFS 2.2 storage chips.

Final words, our verdict, pros and cons

The competition

Back when it originally launched, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G (not to be confused with the 5G version) had a price of €349/£249 for the 8/256GB trim. However, the only widely available units appeared to be 12GB/512GB with price tags of around €400. Currently, as of the time of publishing, you can find the 8GB/256GB for as low as €230.

Now, on the surface, that seems like a pretty good deal. However, it is hard to ignore that you can actually get a lot of phone for that money nowadays. Granted, there will definitely have to be some compromises, but 5G doesn't really have to be one of them, and the lack of modern connectivity is probably the biggest criticism prospective buyers have towards the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G in the first place.

So, why not consider, say, the Redmi Note 15 5G? It costs almost exactly as much. Beyond 5G, it also gets a Snapdragon 6 Gen 3 chipset, which is notably better than the Helio G200 Ultra powering the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G. And it's not only about raw computational power either, but the Snapdragon has a decidedly better ISP - one that allows for 4K video capture instead of 1080p. Other than that, you get a very similar display, though one complete with HDR10+ certification on the Redmi Note 15 5G. You do have to live with a smaller 5,520 mAh battery, though.

Xiaomi Redmi Note 15 • Samsung Galaxy A36 • Motorola Edge 60 Fusion

Over at camp Samsung, you can easily get a base Galaxy A27 for bout as much as the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G. Though unless you really need the SD card slot, you'd be better off with the Galaxy A36 while its stocks last. And don't get us wrong, it's not like the A27 is bad, but with the A36, you get proper IP67 ingress protection, stereo speakers, a brighter display, and noticeably faster charging.

Compared to the Redmi, some highlights include an IP68/IP69 ingress protection rating, a much nicer and brighter HDR10+ certified display, a more powerful chipset, not limited to 1080p video capture and faster charging.

Our verdict

Just like when it launched, now, several months later, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G remains a phone that's hard to recommend. On its own merits, it is a perfectly competent budget-friendly phone. It offers a bright and pleasant AMOLED display, excellent battery life from its large 6,500mAh Si/C battery, dependable cameras, solid build quality with Gorilla Glass Victus 2, stereo speakers, expandable storage and Xiaomi's familiar HyperOS experience backed by a respectable software support commitment.

The problem is not what the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G does well, but rather what Xiaomi chose to leave out. The Helio G200 Ultra already felt dated when it launched, and now holds the entire package back. Performance is merely adequate, gaming potential is limited, and perhaps more importantly, the chipset still cannot record 4K video. Add the complete lack of 5G connectivity, slower-than-expected charging despite the 45W rating, and the phone starts feeling like yesterday's hardware wrapped in a modern shell.

At around €230, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G isn't outrageously priced, but the competition has become exceptionally fierce. Several similarly priced rivals offer substantially faster chipsets, 5G connectivity, HDR-capable displays, stronger ingress protection or much better charging without asking you to make compromises that feel increasingly unnecessary in 2026.

If expandable storage and exceptional battery endurance rank above everything else on your priority list, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G remains a sensible purchase. For everyone else, however, there are simply too many alternatives that deliver a more complete experience for about the same amount of money.

Speakers - loudness and quality

The Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G comes equipped with a stereo speaker setup, consisting of a bottom-firing main unit and a secondary top-firing speaker. The two don't appear to be perfectly matched in size, and that asymmetry is noticeable in real-world use.

That said, overall balance is quite respectable. The phone delivers a reasonably wide soundstage with pleasingly rich mids, making vocals and dialogue sound clear and natural. As expected for a device of this class, bass is essentially absent, and at higher volume levels, the treble becomes overly aggressive, introducing audible distortion.

It seems Xiaomi has prioritized sheer loudness over absolute sound fidelity. The volume slider can be pushed all the way up to a rather extreme 400%, but for clean and comfortable listening, keeping it at or below the standard 100% level is strongly recommended. While the speakers do get impressively loud at maximum output, the resulting sound is far from enjoyable.

As its name suggests, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G is a 4G-only device. At least you get dual SIM support. This includes two physical Nano-SIM cards. As per our testing, there is no eSIM support, but that might be market-dependent. Check with your local retailer.

Positioning includes GPS, GALILEO, GLONASS, QZSS and BDS support. For local connectivity, the phone has dual-band Wi-Fi AC/5, which is a step down from what the 5G model offers. The same goes for the Bluetooth 5.3 support, instead of 5.4. At least you still get Bluetooth LE. Some markets get NFC as well, but you have to check with your retailer of choice for that. There is no 3.5 mm audio jack and no FM radio receiver. You do, however, get an IR blaster located on the top frame.

The Type-C port is backed up by a basic USB 2.0 data connection, which means a maximum theoretical data throughput of 480 Mbps. USB OTG/Host Mode is supported, but there are no really fancy features like video output over USB Alt mode.

For sensors, you get an ST lsm6dsvqtr accelerometer and gyroscope combo, a QST qmc6308 magnetometer and compass combo, an SI sip1328 light sensor and what is unfortunately a virtual Elliptic Labs proximity sensor. It works reliably enough for turning the display off during calls, which is at least a positive.

HyperOS is moving forward

Benchmarks and performance

We have arguably arrived at the Achilles' heel of the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G. The MediaTek Helio G200 Ultra is simply put underwhelming in terms of performance, no two ways about it. The G200 is essentially the G100, which in turn is essentially the G99, and none of these is impressive in any manner whatsoever.

Despite being a 2025 model, as we said, the G200 Ultra is essentially a rebranded entry-level chipset from 2022. While its 6nm TSMC manufacturing node is modern enough by current standards, the actual hardware here is pretty bad. The CPU setup consists of a retro two-plus-four core configuration, with two Cortex-A76 units, working at up to 2.2 GHz and another six Cortex-A55 units, clocked at up to 2.0 GHz. In case you were wondering, the Cortex-A55 was launched in 2017.

Things don't get better in the GPU department, where the G200 Ultra gets a slightly overclocked Mali-G57 MC2, working at up to 1.1 GHz. Again, a pretty aging part that was never meant to be impressive to begin with and one that has really started showing its cracks.

Credit where credit is due, the Helio G100 and G200 do introduce a slightly upgraded ISP that handles up to a 200MP single camera sensor, for what that's worth, since video capture is still capped at 1080p. Major disappointment there.

Actual benchmark results only put the disappointment into perspective. You are essentially getting the same lackluster performance as last year's Redmi Note 14 Pro 4G, and it's not like HyperOS has gotten any lighter in that time. The closest comparable chipset here is the Snapdragon 6s Gen 3, and it's not a shining beacon of performance either.

The global Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G model can be had in any of the following memory configurations: 256GB/8GB, 256GB/12GB and 512GB/12GB. All of the tiers include dual-channel LPDDR4X RAM and UFS 2.2 storage chips.

Camera - photo and video quality

Two cameras on the back, the usual 32MP selfies

If you were hoping that Xiaomi made some sweeping changes to the camera setup since the previous model, we would ask you to define "sweeping" since the 2MP dedicated macro cam is gone (good riddance), which does constitute a change. Joking aside, you get essentially the same dual camera setup as the previous-gen Redmi Note 14 Pro 4G (and sort of similar to the same-gen Redmi Note 15 Pro 5G).

Despite having four circles on its camera island, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G essentially has two cameras - a primary wide one and an ultrawide. The main 200MP camera is based on the Samsung ISOCELL S5KHP3 - the generic and more widely available version to the HPE that the Redmi Note 15 Pro 5G reports (and we gather is a customized Xiaomi-specific version).

The ultrawide camera, while still 8MP in resolution, is a bit different than the one used in the 5G model - here it's a GalaxyCore sensor in place of the OmniVision one found there.

The front camera is not an exact match to the one on the Redmi Note 15 Pro 5G either. It is a 32MP unit based on the OmniVision OV32D.

So, bottom line, the camera setup on the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G is mostly borrowed from the Redmi Note 14 Pro 4G, rather than the Redmi Note 15 Pro 5G, even though there are some similarities at first glance.

And, we keep circling back to this, but we just can't help ourselves - the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G is limited to 1080p video capture due to its lackluster chipset.

  • Ultrawide: 8MP GalaxyCore GC08A8 (1/4.0", 1.12µm); 15mm, f/2.2, fixed focus; 1080p30.

Final words, our verdict, pros and cons

The competition

Back when it originally launched, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G (not to be confused with the 5G version) had a price of €349/£249 for the 8/256GB trim. However, the only widely available units appeared to be 12GB/512GB with price tags of around €400. Currently, as of the time of publishing, you can find the 8GB/256GB for as low as €230.

Now, on the surface, that seems like a pretty good deal. However, it is hard to ignore that you can actually get a lot of phone for that money nowadays. Granted, there will definitely have to be some compromises, but 5G doesn't really have to be one of them, and the lack of modern connectivity is probably the biggest criticism prospective buyers have towards the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G in the first place.

So, why not consider, say, the Redmi Note 15 5G? It costs almost exactly as much. Beyond 5G, it also gets a Snapdragon 6 Gen 3 chipset, which is notably better than the Helio G200 Ultra powering the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G. And it's not only about raw computational power either, but the Snapdragon has a decidedly better ISP - one that allows for 4K video capture instead of 1080p. Other than that, you get a very similar display, though one complete with HDR10+ certification on the Redmi Note 15 5G. You do have to live with a smaller 5,520 mAh battery, though.

Xiaomi Redmi Note 15 • Samsung Galaxy A36 • Motorola Edge 60 Fusion

Over at camp Samsung, you can easily get a base Galaxy A27 for bout as much as the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G. Though unless you really need the SD card slot, you'd be better off with the Galaxy A36 while its stocks last. And don't get us wrong, it's not like the A27 is bad, but with the A36, you get proper IP67 ingress protection, stereo speakers, a brighter display, and noticeably faster charging.

If you shop smart and don't insist on Motorola's newest Edge 70 family, you can get an Edge 70 Fusion that gets you most of the way there anyway. Compared to the Redmi, some highlights include an IP68/IP69 ingress protection rating, a much nicer and brighter HDR10+ certified display, a more powerful chipset, not limited to 1080p video capture and faster charging.

Our verdict

Just like when it launched, now, several months later, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G remains a phone that's hard to recommend. On its own merits, it is a perfectly competent budget-friendly phone. It offers a bright and pleasant AMOLED display, excellent battery life from its large 6,500mAh Si/C battery, dependable cameras, solid build quality with Gorilla Glass Victus 2, stereo speakers, expandable storage and Xiaomi's familiar HyperOS experience backed by a respectable software support commitment.

The problem is not what the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G does well, but rather what Xiaomi chose to leave out. The Helio G200 Ultra already felt dated when it launched, and now holds the entire package back. Performance is merely adequate, gaming potential is limited, and perhaps more importantly, the chipset still cannot record 4K video. Add the complete lack of 5G connectivity, slower-than-expected charging despite the 45W rating, and the phone starts feeling like yesterday's hardware wrapped in a modern shell.

At around €230, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G isn't outrageously priced, but the competition has become exceptionally fierce. Several similarly priced rivals offer substantially faster chipsets, 5G connectivity, HDR-capable displays, stronger ingress protection or much better charging without asking you to make compromises that feel increasingly unnecessary in 2026.

If expandable storage and exceptional battery endurance rank above everything else on your priority list, the Redmi Note 15 Pro 4G remains a sensible purchase. For everyone else, however, there are simply too many alternatives that deliver a more complete experience for about the same amount of money.
