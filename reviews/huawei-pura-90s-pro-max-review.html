Introduction, specs, unboxing

Introduction

US sanctions may have pushed Huawei out of the spotlight in the international smartphone market, but the company remains a dominant force at home. It continues to lead the Chinese market and even posted its highest market share in years in the latest quarter, according to Counterpoint.

Every now and then, one of Huawei's flagship phones makes it beyond China's borders, serving both its loyal global fanbase and as a reminder of what the company is still capable of. The Pura 90s Pro Max is one of those rare releases - a close relative of the China-exclusive Pura 90 Pro Max.

Huawei touts the Pura 90s Pro Max as a special edition of the Chinese model, made for the true fans of the Pura series. It is also branded as a holiday phone, with fresh exterior colors and stand-out cameras.

But let's start from the beginning. The Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max is the current flagship of the photo-centric series. It's a large glass slab with a signature triangular camera arrangement on the back, one that is as large as a FreeBuds 7i case, for example.

The Pura 90s Pro Max uses a 6.9-inch LTPO OLED screen with 1,308p resolution, 10-bit color depth, and HDR support. It is protected by the latest Kunlun glass with anti-glare and anti-fingerprint properties.

Then there is the Kirin 9030S chip, which has switched to a 5nm node. It still offers mid-range-level 8-core CPU and GPU, but it's paired with 12GB LPDDR5X RAM and UFS storage. Plus, there is 5G support on the Pura 90s Pro Max even on the global unit, which means Huawei has successfully navigated the restrictions imposed by the US.

The centerpiece of the Pura 90s Pro Max is the camera system, of course. It packs a 50MP primary camera with a variable aperture, a 40MP ultrawide-angle camera with autofocus and macro support, and a 200MP telephoto/telemacro camera with 4x optical zoom and up to 20x lossless zoom (works even in videos). There are additional helper systems such as laser assistance and a color spectrum sensor.

The selfie camera is a 13MP ultrawide-angle one with autofocus.

We mentioned that the Pura 90s Pro Max works on 5G networks, but it can also brag with Bluetooth 6.0, Wi-Fi 7, NFC, IR, and all sorts of GPS positioning support. The satellite communication is reserved for the Chinese unit, though.

Finally, the global units of the Pura 90s Pro Max come with a battery that's rated as 5,270mAh (which should mean a typical capacity between 5,400-5,500mAh), while the Chinese model has a 5,850mAh rated battery (typical capacity 6,000mAh). Both versions support 100W wired and 80W wireless charging.

The global model of the Pura 90s Pro Max boots EMUI 16 with Android 16 core inside, which is a new development again. All past EMUI versions in the recent past were based on Android 12. Of course, there are still no Google Mobile Services preinstalled. But there are plenty of workarounds for that.

Design, build quality, handling

Design, build quality, handling

The Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max is easily recognizable for its signature camera housing on the back, one that has been growing steadily since its introduction on the first Pura 70 series. Other than this trait, the new Pura is your typical dual-glass smartphone with an aluminum frame in between.

The Pura 90s Pro Max comes in four different colors, two of which are quite fresh and feature what Huawei claims to be an industry-first: Dual-Tone Gradient Mid-Frame. It certainly brought memories of the dual-tone frame of the RedMagic 5s, but we can't be sure if the dual-tone finish is technically the same.

The two versions with this finish are Orange Ocean and Blaze Purple, which are in line with the Pura 90s Pro Max holiday aura and we want to quote the maker on the rest - the Pura 90s Pro Max "draws its aesthetic palette from holiday tranquility" and that the phone "exudes effortless vacation ease" and also "embodies travel romance through a vivid spectrum of captivating hues". Well, we'd say mission accomplished.

We have the Graphite Black model for review, which is less adventurous and more stylish. The same can be said about the Blush Gold colorway, which is also boring, just brighter. There are no gradients on these two colorways.

The Pura 90s Pro Max is IP68 and IP69 rated for dust, water and pressurized water jet resistance. It's rated to withstand depths up to 2m for 30min.

The Kunlun front glass is also advertised for its scratch and drop resistance.

The Pura 90s Pro Max is a flat device on both sides, but the triangular camera housing is about half a centimeter thick, and the phone can never really lie flat on a desk. At least it doesn't wobble with or without a case.

The camera island is unique, and it has this volcano-like shape rising from the back glass. And while the housing is a separate piece from the back, you have to look really close to see that.

And now, let's look at the Pura 90s Pro Max from up close.

The front has the 6.9-inch LTPO OLED screen with impressively thin bezels. It has a small perforation for the 13MP ultrawide-angle selfie camera.

The earpiece is almost invisible near the top part of the frame, and it also doubles as a stereo speaker. It has a secondary top-facing outlet, but its position is quite peculiar.

The back has the camera housing, with three separate rings for each camera - the 50MP primary, the 200MP telephoto, and the 40MP ultrawide. The rings of the ultrawide and telephoto camera contain the almost invisible color spectrum sensor and laser emitter and transmitter. Finally, the LED flash is also around, outside those rings.

The aluminum frame is polished and quite slippery. It's also quite easy to smudge up on this black version of the Pura 90s Pro Max.

There is nothing to note on the left-hand side.

The right side houses the volume and power key. The latter also incorporates the fingerprint scanner on its surface. The sensor is always-on and accurate as you'd expect it to be.

The bottom has the primary mic, the USB-C port, the dual-SIM tray, and the other speaker.

The Pura 90s Pro Max measures 164 x 77.1 x 8.1 mm and weighs 231 grams, which is about the same size and weight as the iPhone 17 Pro Max. A heavy slab for sure, but not an overweight one.

The phone is made of glass and polished aluminum, which makes it incredibly slippery. Fingerprints and smudges stick out a lot, too.

Overall, the Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max is a beautiful device, that's for sure. It is easily recognizable, features a premium build and is promised to be tough. The gargantuan camera housing may not be everyone's cup of tea, but we think most people won't mind it after they discover its prowess.

Kunlun Glass keeps the display panel safe.

The only official specs Huawei has shared besides the resolution are support for a dynamic 1Hz-120Hz refresh rate, up to 300Hz touch sampling, and high-frequency PWM dimming.

We measured 1,192 nits of maximum brightness on the Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max display with our usual 75% white patch displayed. When using a 10% white patch, brightness peaked at 1,777nits. Those are solid numbers, though not the best we've seen.

The minimum brightness was just 1 nit, an excellent performance.

Refresh rate

As per the official pages, the Pura 90s Pro Max supports dynamic refresh rate between 1Hz and 120Hz. The available user options are High mode (up to 120Hz with using the maximum possible RR), a Standard mode (up to 60Hz), and a Dynamic mode (up to 120Hz, but smart switching for longer battery life).

We saw the refresh rate drop down to 60Hz when idling and in certain scenarios such as video streaming. If you have Dynamic mode on, you may see 90Hz across a limited number of apps.

The refresh rate tracker integrated in Android showed only 60Hz, 90Hz and 120Hz, so we cannot really confirm if the screen can really drop down to 1Hz.

HDR and streaming

The display of the Pura 90s Pro Max supports HDR10 as per the hardware scanning apps. YouTube (via GBox) successfully streams HDR clips on the phone. Netflix was also able to stream FullHD HDR10 movies for us when installed through GBox. Nice!

Battery life

The Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max battery capacity varies depending on the region. The Chinese unit gets a 5,850mAh rated battery (typical 6,000mAh), while the European one has a 5,270mAh rated capcity (unknown typical value but our best guess is anywhere between 5,400-5,500mAh). The phone itself reports 5,550mAh capacity if that any indication.

In our set of battery tests, the phone posted a good overall Active Use Rating of 13:02 hours. It did very well on the call scenario, the web browsing and especially on the video streaming test, but the gaming runtime was only average.

Charging test

The Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max supports 100W fast wired and 80W fast wireless charging. It can also do reverse wired and wireless charging.

The phone should ship with a 100W SuperCharge power adapter, though we didn't get one. Still, we had one from before, so we were able to test the charging speed with a proper adapter.

Unfortunately, the Pura 90s Pro Max was not very fast to charge - it went from 1% to 100% in 61 minutes. We also clocked 39% on the 15min mark and 60% of charge on the 30min mark. For some reason, that's slower than the Pura 80 Ultra charging speed, a phone with a similar battery and charging support.

Software, performance

EMUI 16 with Android 16 core

The global version of the Huawei Pura 90s Pro boots Android 16 with EMUI 16. That's an update over previous versions of EMUI that were based on much older Android cores. EMUI supports APK and Android apps, quite expectedly, and it relies on HMS - Huawei Mobile Services, instead of GMS - Google Mobile Services.

In contrast, the Chinese model runs on Huawei's own HarmonyOS, which is an independent operating system that does not rely on the Android Open Source Project. HarmonyOS has its own microkernel and app format. That shouldn't be confused with HarmonyOS as found on Huawei's globally available tablets, as that OS is more or less like the international EMUI and still based on Android.

The new EMUI 16, which we see here, not only brings a newer underlying Android version, but also Celia AI and Celia one-command ability.

EMUI looks quite familiar as it has not changed drastically over the years. It includes neat little things like large folders and cards - another name for the widgets that you can add for the in-house apps, indicated by a bar under the app icon.

Air gestures and smart sensing are supported on the Pura 90s Pro Max - a familiar capability also found on previous Huawei and Honor models.

EMUI 16

Huawei offers a lot of default apps to get you started. There are Huawei's Gallery, Music, Video, and Health apps. A File Manager app is available, as well. There is also Smart Remote - you can turn your phone into a universal remote thanks to its IR blaster.

You also get Petal Maps, Petal Search and Huawei's own Browser.

Huawei's AppGallery handles the app installations by default, and it seems the advertisements and pop-ups have been drastically lowered since EMUI 14.

You can install other app repositories, and they work just fine.

Performance and benchmarks

The Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max is powered by a new HiSilicon Kirin 9030S chip, a platform that has been based on SMIC's 5nm-class FinFET node known as the N+3 process. The 9030S seems to be both a downgrade and an upgrade compared to the Kirin 9030 Pro inside the Mate X7.

The octa-core processor, for example, has been downclocked compared to the Mate X7. There is a single Taishan V124 Big core, clocked at up to 2.7 GHz, three Taishan V124 Mid cores, working at up to 2.15 GHz and four Taishan V124 Little cores, limited to 1.62 GHz.

The HiSilicon Maleoon 935F GPU though is more baffling. It seems to be rated for around 1433 GFLOPS of performance, compared to 2388 GFLOPS on the regular Kirin 9030's GPU and 2866 GFLOPS on the Pro model. So, we expect notably worse GPU performance here.

The chip is coupled with either 12GB LPDDR5X RAM and 256GB or 512GB UFS storage.

The Kirin 9030S supports 5G connectivity and eSIM.

Quite expectedly, the Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max scores as low as the Snapdragon 7 Gen 4 inside the Motorola Edge 70 - a lower-tier mid-range chipset.

And, as expected, the performance of the GPU inside the Pura 90s Pro Max has been halved compared to the Kirin 9030 Pro inside the Mate X7.

Camera - photo and video quality

New fixed-lens telephoto, main camera loses the 1.0" type sensor

The highest-tier Pura for this year bridges the gap between last year's Pro+ and Ultra and streamlines a few hardware aspects. The mostly gimmicky (if intriguing in principle) switchable lens telephoto of the Ultra has been replaced by a new, more conventional solution, that's also quite superior to the Pro+'s setup. The main camera, meanwhile, has gotten a smaller sensor (1-inchers have been steadily disappearing), but gets to keep the variable aperture.

The new telephoto camera uses a 200MP 1/1.28" sensor - same size as the one in the Pura 80 Ultra which is also a tie for the largest sensor in a telephoto camera with the Oppo Find X9 Ultra. It's not the same OmniVision OV52A imager found on the Oppo, however, and it's seemingly not the one from the Pura 80 Ultra either, since Huawei is explicitly stating that theirs has an RYYB subpixel array and it's the first of that kind in the industry (Huawei has a long history of preferring Yellow sub-pixels over Green). A certain SmartSense model was floated in earlier reports, called the SCC80XS, but we currently have no way of confirming it.

The lens is longer than the one on the Find, with the Pura 90s Pro Max offering 4x zoom (3x on the Oppo), with the usual caveat that Huawei focal lengths are never what they appear. Here, the phone reports an 89mm equivalent in hardware apps, while the photos have 96mm in the respective EXIF field. Either way, the lens is stabilized and is CIPA-rated for up to 7 stops of stabilization.

The Pura 80 Ultra's optical zoom-in functionality (which itself wasn't entirely honest) is gone for the 90s Pro Max, and getting more reach beyond the nominal 4x will require some in-sensor remosaicking action, which the 200MP imager should be well-equipped to deliver. Why they insist on 10x instead of 8x, remains a mystery.

On the main camera, we're looking at another 1/1.28" sensor, only this time it's a 50MP one. It's also got an RYYB subpixel arrangement, of course. Now, the previous generation had 1.0" type sensors on the main cameras on all but the vanilla model (so the Pro, Pro+ and Ultra), but this time it's a new LOFIC (Lateral Overflow Integration Capacitor) type sensor, so it should be at least as good, maybe. The lens has OIS and a variable aperture in the f/1.4-f/4.0 range (another typical Huawei feature).

The trio of cameras on the back is completed by the ultrawide that has a 13mm equivalent lens with an f/2.2 aperture and autofocus. The sensor is also a Huawei staple - a 40MP RYYB unit.

There's also what Huawei calls True-to-Color camera, which is a dedicated spectral sensor for more granular color temperature sensing and accurate white balance in complex lighting. They're claiming a 43% improvement in 'color restoration accuracy' compared to the Pura 80 generation, too. Specific scenes should be rendered a lot better than on rivals, they say, like high saturation mixed lighting (a pretty extreme pink/orange/purple neon test setup was shown) and high saturation blue lighting (think of an underwater aquarium).

Camera - photo and video quality

New fixed-lens telephoto, main camera loses the 1.0" type sensor

The highest-tier Pura for this year bridges the gap between last year's Pro+ and Ultra and streamlines a few hardware aspects. The mostly gimmicky (if intriguing in principle) switchable lens telephoto of the Ultra has been replaced by a new, more conventional solution, that's also quite superior to the Pro+'s setup. The main camera, meanwhile, has gotten a smaller sensor (1-inchers have been steadily disappearing), but gets to keep the variable aperture.

The new telephoto camera uses a 200MP 1/1.28" sensor - same size as the one in the Pura 80 Ultra which is also a tie for the largest sensor in a telephoto camera with the Oppo Find X9 Ultra. It's not the same OmniVision OV52A imager found on the Oppo, however, and it's seemingly not the one from the Pura 80 Ultra either, since Huawei is explicitly stating that theirs has an RYYB subpixel array and it's the first of that kind in the industry (Huawei has a long history of preferring Yellow sub-pixels over Green). A certain SmartSense model was floated in earlier reports, called the SCC80XS, but we currently have no way of confirming it.

The lens is longer than the one on the Find, with the Pura 90s Pro Max offering 4x zoom (3x on the Oppo), with the usual caveat that Huawei focal lengths are never what they appear. Here, the phone reports an 89mm equivalent in hardware apps, while the photos have 96mm in the respective EXIF field. Either way, the lens is stabilized and is CIPA-rated for up to 7 stops of stabilization.

The Pura 80 Ultra's optical zoom-in functionality (which itself wasn't entirely honest) is gone for the 90s Pro Max, and getting more reach beyond the nominal 4x will require some in-sensor remosaicking action, which the 200MP imager should be well-equipped to deliver. Why they insist on 10x instead of 8x, remains a mystery.

On the main camera, we're looking at another 1/1.28" sensor, only this time it's a 50MP one. It's also got an RYYB subpixel arrangement, of course. Now, the previous generation had 1.0" type sensors on the main cameras on all but the vanilla model (so the Pro, Pro+ and Ultra), but this time it's a new LOFIC (Lateral Overflow Integration Capacitor) type sensor, so it should be at least as good, maybe. The lens has OIS and a variable aperture in the f/1.4-f/4.0 range (another typical Huawei feature).

The trio of cameras on the back is completed by the ultrawide that has a 13mm equivalent lens with an f/2.2 aperture and autofocus. The sensor is also a Huawei staple - a 40MP RYYB unit.

There's also what Huawei calls True-to-Color camera, which is a dedicated spectral sensor for more granular color temperature sensing and accurate white balance in complex lighting. They're claiming a 43% improvement in 'color restoration accuracy' compared to the Pura 80 generation, too. Specific scenes should be rendered a lot better than on rivals, they say, like high saturation mixed lighting (a pretty extreme pink/orange/purple neon test setup was shown) and high saturation blue lighting (think of an underwater aquarium).

Over on the front, there's a fairly modest 13MP selfie camera - same one as on the entire previous generation in the lineup. It's still got a wide 21mm lens and it has autofocus, so it's not entirely basic - it's just the sensor that sounds a little old-school at first glance.

The Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max is a mightily capable photo camera. The 4x telephoto is also top-class at its nominal focal length and quite good at twice that, while the 10x zoom they're trying to force on you is more questionable, but still alright. The ultrawide does well during the day and mostly okay at night. None of the three cameras exactly impresses in the 25MP mode, but you could probably find uses for that too. Selfies are excellent too.

Video recording

The Pura 90s Pro Max can record video up to 4K60 with all four of its cameras. There's no 8K option, and there's no 24fps setting for 4K either, not even in the (relatively unremarkable) Pro mode. HDR Vivid recording is available up to 4K30 (but not in Pro mode, where it's grayed out), and there's no Log option in either mode. The default codec is h.265 (HEVC) but you can switch back to h.264.

The video stabilization approach remains unchanged - it's always on for all modes, and there's also a Steady shot mode that caps the resolution at 2.7K (at either 30fps or 60fps).

Video quality out of the Pura 90s Pro Max is very good. The main camera's detail is very good at 1x and actually pretty decent at 2x too, the telephoto is good at 4x and so-so at 10x, and the ultrawide is pretty good in its own context too. Huaweis have too often left us unimpressed with their video quality in the past for us to expect greatness now, but greatness is almost what the Pura 90s Pro Max gave us. The competition, our verdict, pros and cons

The competition

The Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max may not win any awards for its name, but it makes a strong first impression with its unmistakable design and unconventional camera setup. More importantly, that camera hardware delivers excellent photo and video quality.

Unfortunately, there's more to a flagship than good looks and great cameras. While the Pura 90s Pro Max impresses with its design, display, and imaging capabilities, its chipset and software are likely to be deal-breakers for many buyers. The €1,199 asking price doesn't do it any favors either.

Let's see what the competition has to offer.

Naturally, the first that comes to mind is the Xiaomi 17 Ultra. It features the most powerful Snapdragon chip, much faster than the Kirin inside the Huawei, and comes with a better screen, cameras (with continuous zoom), and battery life. Let's not forget the complete Android OS with all Google services. All for the same asking price or lower than the Huawei's proposition.

Then there is the Oppo Find X9 Ultra, which also impresses with flagship-grade design, screen, and Elite chip. Its camera department has two telephoto cameras for better versatility and a high-resolution selfie imager for better photo quality. Just like the Xiaomi 17 Ultra, the Find X9 Ultra is one of the best phones on the market, but it's quite a bit more expensive at €1500.

Of course, we cannot help but mention the vivo X300 Ultra, which is even more expensive than the Find X9 Ultra but is an equally good cameraphone (or even better, depending on who you ask).

The one thing the Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max has that the rest don't - it's the variable aperture on the main camera. However, the benefits of that feature outside a few very specific use cases are dubious.

Our verdict

The Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max is a solid smartphone - it impressed us with its design, it has a great screen for multimedia, and the speakers are quite good, too.

The Pura 90s Pro Max offers a versatile camera set with excellent photo and video quality across the board. We do appreciate the effort to bring 10x magnification, but we think settling for the maximum possible of 8x would have been much better. At least the phone deserves an 8x shortcut inside the camera app.

The selfies turned out to be very good, too, even if coming from a more basic sensor.

The Kirin chip is still a mid-range one, worse, its GPU performance has gone downhill since the last Huawei we've tested - the Mate X7. And while it will do just fine for the occasional game, it's just not worthy for a flagship phone with a €1000+ price tag. At least the connectivity department got an upgrade with 5G and Wi-Fi 7.

And then comes the EMUI, the universal dilemma of leaving behind everything Google. Can you live with it? Well, for most people the answer will be positive. First, it's got an Android 16 core, and you can install pretty much anything on it. Then GBox allows you to get GMS and use your favorite GMS-reliant apps. EMUI is also quite powerful in terms of customizations and features.

Sooner or later, though, you'll run into an app that simply won't work. And not everyone wants to deal with downloading and installing apps from outside the official app store. As things stand, Huawei's phones still lack broad mainstream appeal and are likely to find their audience among users who deliberately choose to stay outside Google's ecosystem.

Overall, the Huawei Pura 90s Pro Max is an intriguing smartphone, but it's also one with a very specific target audience. If its camera system is your top priority, its compromises may be easy to forgive. If not, there are more complete flagship experiences available for the same money.
